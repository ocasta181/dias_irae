# Dias Irae — Locations

Rooms, roads, and undercrofts of the campaign. What happens in them: [story-arch.md](story-arch.md). Who stands in them: [characters.md](characters.md).

The campaign map is France — the Royans, the Isère, the Drôme, the Rhône corridor. The East is memory. Visual law for every place: Romanesque churches, limewash, barrel vaults, small windows, painted Christs in Majesty. Mail and kite shields in the yards. The later Isenheim Altarpiece is a visual inspiration; the stones themselves are 1101. This remains the working map, with local ruin invented for the campaign. The arc of restored Christian hope can work in another setting if the author chooses one.

---

## The land in one look

The Royans is a limestone country of gorges and hanging terraces, the Vercors rising east, the Isère opening west toward the Rhône. Rye on the benches. Vines where the sun holds. Villages pulled tight around a church and a mill. Summer 1101 should feel like a harvest that has decided to be something else: gold gone to charcoal, white dust, a sweet rot under ordinary farm smells. Sky often low. When it clears, the light is hard and shows too much.

The blight follows grain routes and pilgrimage roads. It appears first where contaminated provisions are made and distributed. False remedies around the counterfeit exploit holy appearances. Genuine blessing does not cause the blight.

Palette for the whole game: rye-gold, charcoal, limewash, Antonine black and taupe, dried-blood wine, pig-fat yellow, the dull white of bone prostheses, the iron of a millstone.

Churches are small, dim, painted, damp. Hospitals show fear and corruption, but also competent care and courageous service. The fields are wrong. The counterfeit borrows sacred appearances. Antioch, Jerusalem, and the road home are places Guarin remembers. The precise mithraeum beneath the hospital is fictional archaeology.

Goodness must have a visible place in the map: shelter freely offered, people sharing food, patients helping one another, and work resuming where Guarin has stopped harm. Return visits can show durable recovery. Rain at the stone cross is fictional scene weather, not a claim about a recorded storm in 1101.

# Act I — The Unkept House

## The last league into the Royans

**Function.** Arrival. Tutorial landscape. The first signs of the suffering that deepens Guarin's spiritual crisis.

**History.** The old path down from the Vercors edge toward the Bourne, used by drovers and by anyone bringing a coffin to Saint-Laurent. Guarin walked it in the other direction in 1096.

**Visual style.** Switchback above a gorge, pale rock, box and juniper. Then the first terraces. The rye is standing. From a distance it is a harvest. Closer, the heads are swollen, dark, curved like cockspurs. A roadside cross has a sooty film on the Christ, as if a lamp had smoked against it for a year. A shoe. A dropped basket of heads the birds have left.

**Feel.** Wind in the gorge, then a hush of insects. The mule baulks at the last mile. Dust tastes sweet.

**Story use.** Establish the wrong grain and the people affected by it. Guarin can take a head of rye as evidence. It does not turn his devotional splinter into a key for the enemy.

---

## The stone cross

**Function.** Required early prayer scene. Guarin names his despair and asks Christ for will, conviction, guidance, and strength.

**History.** A local stone cross near the approach to Rivoire, known to him before he left. Its particular form and location are fictional.

**Visual style.** Worn stone, wet grass, mud under his knees. Rain runs over the cross and beneath his collar. His helmet lies beside him. He cries openly; his shoulders shake and his hand rests against the stone.

**Feel.** Rain, uneven breathing, and a voice faltering through tears. Give the prayer time. His sincerity is the scene's center.

**Story use.** He says the prayer preserved verbatim in the story outline. He wants the strength of will to continue the fight and the strength of conviction to have hope. He is not praying for shelter. The journey fulfills the prayer as he sees goodness, life's worth, and the meaning of his struggle again. The particular form of a visible divine response remains an authorial choice.

## The village of Rivoire

**Function.** Hub of Act I. The parish as first dungeon and first home.

**History.** A minor holding of the castellans of Rivoire, owing to Albon. Forty fires in a good year, fewer now. Saint-Laurent, a mill on the stream, a bake-oven, a well, a lychgate. Guarin was baptized here. Half the men his age went east. Some came back. The miller died in Giraud's fire.

**Visual style.** Tight cluster of limewashed houses gone to ochre and soot. Roofs of stone slab. Mud packed with chaff. The village cross in the place is blackened as if by a lamp, the same film as the roadside Christ. Doors shut at noon. One house has a tau marked on it in charcoal, a sign the parish leaves unexplained. Empty chicken yards. A dog that stays put.

**Feel.** A silence with work still in it. Smoke from one chimney. Someone muttering Latin behind a door, the cases wrong. Smell of burnt grain over ordinary dung and whey.

**Inhabitants.** The remaining tenants; the burned and the merely hungry; wolves at the paddock after dark. People Guarin can name.

**Story use.** First encounters are among people Guarin knows. Distinguish hostile or possessed people from those who need help. Return hub between acts: the advancing blight can worsen it, while aid and protection create lasting signs of recovery.

---

## Church of Saint-Laurent

**Function.** Parish church. A place for prayer and renewed service. Giraud's folded chasuble.

**History.** A small Romanesque church of the last century, one aisle, a painted Christ in Majesty in the apse that local hands redo when the damp takes the plaster. Aimar is in the yard, twice. The lychgate is where Agnes recites.

**Visual style.** Thick walls, slit windows, a dim nave that smells of old incense and wet stone. The apse Christ is still correct. The processional crucifix on its beam has grown a second, smaller crossbar — a tau — as if the wood had always had it. The aumbry is locked. Giraud's chasuble is folded in a chest in the sacristy. The font is dry. Someone has left rye-heads on the altar step as if they were flowers.

**Feel.** Cool, with an edge. Sound of the stream through the south wall. The crucifix seems nearer than the nave is long.

**Story use.** Agnes at the lychgate. Giraud in the sacristy or the yard. Giraud and Odo's observations lead toward Raimon in the undercroft. Prayer can steady Guarin. Giraud has withdrawn from celebrating the Mass in fear; helping him face the miller's death and resume service restores a place of worship for the parish. Communion is not a trap that exposes Guarin's hidden guilt.

**Yard.** Two graves for one man, the second shallower, stones on the lid. Other new mounds. The ground is busy.

---

## The mill

**Function.** Crime scene of Giraud's wrong mercy. First look at the sclerotia.

**History.** The miller of Rivoire ground the parish's rye and a little wheat. In the spring, after the crucifix changed, Giraud burned it. The miller was inside, trying to save the stone. The stone survived. The building is a shell.

**Visual style.** A wet charcoal shell over a stream. The wheel is jammed with black clots. The millstone is webbed with sclerotia — dark, spur-shaped, slightly glossy. When smashed they bleed a thick rust-colored moisture. A child's sweep-brush, still pale, in a corner Agnes leaves alone.

**Feel.** Wet ash, sweet rot, cold water. The birds have gone. The wheel ticks when the stream pushes, irregular as a bad pulse.

**Story use.** Evidence. Smashing sclerotia teaches the body of the blight. Agnes stays in the yard. Giraud's palm-scars match the door-iron.

---

## The castrum of Rivoire

**Function.** Home. Almodis's keep. Hub.

**History.** A modest stone hall and tower on a limestone knuckle above the village, 11th-century work on older footings. A yard, a stable, an undercroft, a chapel the size of a room that Aimar used on feast days. Guarin's childhood. Almodis's widowhood. Aimar died in the upper chamber facing the terraces.

**Visual style.** Grey-yellow stone, small windows, a hall with a central hearth and smoke-darkened beams. The shutters are closed in daytime. Almodis keeps the table set for fewer and fewer. Guarin's old corner still has a peg for a belt. The household chapel's crucifix is still a single cross; Almodis would have taken an axe to a second bar. Dust on the lance-rest. The valley scratched into the hearth-stone by a boy who is now thirty-four.

**Feel.** A house being run correctly in a dying place. Woodsmoke, boiled grain, lye. Almodis's voice carries. At night the undercroft talks — apples shifting, or something that uses their sound.

**Inhabitants.** Almodis; the cook; the boy; two old men; until Act I ends, Raimon.

**Story use.** Hub. Almodis is the measure of the valley. The upper chamber holds Aimar's last belt and a letter he left unfinished. The player may return. While Almodis holds it, it remains a house. Aid brought home can preserve people and allow repairs, shared meals, and ordinary work to return.

---

## The undercroft and paddock

**Function.** Act I boss room and its approach.

**History.** Stores, cider, apples, a barred stall for a sick horse. Raimon kept the keys on Aimar's belt. He has been living down here since the legs went, giving reports to the dark.

**Visual style.** Low vaults, barrel, lime flaking. Last year's apples have mummified into black fists. One stall is open. Horse-hide wrappings on a stool. Scratch-marks at knee height that become something else toward the back wall. The paddock above is churned. Wolves by dusk; one stands too long.

**Feel.** Cold sweetness. Raimon's voice counting sheaves. The sound of a gait made for more than two feet.

**Story use.** Raimon the Long. Afterward, if Guarin buries him in the yard himself, the grave holds.

# Act II — The Tau

## The Bourne road and Pont-en-Royans

**Function.** Travel dungeon. Processions. First inhuman things.

**History.** The Bourne cuts a gorge through limestone. The crossing at Pont is older than the houses. Droving, timber, and now pilgrims to Anthony's relics use it. In a good year it is loud. In 1101 the loudness is processional.

**Visual style.** Sheer pale rock, a green river too far down, a stone bridge with chapels at the piles. Houses cluster at the heads of the bridge. Tau-crosses chalked on the parapet. A line of penitents with stripped backs. Terraces of rye above the gorge, the heads moving in still air. Pigs in a riverside pen, mouths that repay a second look.

**Feel.** River-roar that covers other sounds. Cold coming up from the water. Blood-iron from the scourges. Someone singing a psalm on a warped note and keeping on.

**Story use.** Flagellants. Brigands. Rye-men and chaff angels in the terraces. Holy pigs. The flagellant master who named Agnes. A lazaret at the far head of the bridge.

---

## The lazaret on the Pont road

**Function.** Threshold of the abbey. First rumor of Raimbaut.

**History.** A shed-and-chapel for those still waiting on the abbey's beds, or sent back down. Built this summer. Already old.

**Visual style.** Wattle, lime, a tau over the door painted in wine that reads as blood at a glance. Pallets. A bucket of lard. Men with black hands and men with white faces. A priest out of his depth.

**Feel.** Flies. Heat under a roof that should be cold. Revelation available to anyone who looks.

**Story use.** Talk of a knight from Saillans gathering the marked. Optional mercy: bring water, bring a man up the path to La-Motte. Remembered later if done.

---

## Abbey-hospital of Saint Anthony at La-Motte-Saint-Didier

**Function.** Heart of Act II. Institution. Side chapel of the Eastern relic.

**History.** Local tradition places the alms fraternity around 1095, associated with Gaston of Valloire and his son's cure. A monastic shrine and early charitable household supply the historical setting. The mature Antonine religious order belongs later. The hospital's scale, detailed practices, and fictional Prior Isarn are adaptations for this campaign; Gaston remains an offstage patron in the fiction.

**Visual style.** Romanesque shrine-church, low and thick, the tau everywhere — on habits, on the gable, on bread-stamps. Black and taupe cloth on a line. A courtyard of stretchers. Limewashed wards with rows of straw beds. Pig-bone prostheses hanging on pegs like tools. Painted life of Anthony on the nave wall: desert, beasts, fire. The beasts are a little too specific. Smoke from a bakehouse that smells of wine and rye.

**Feel.** Wine, lard, sweet gangrene, incense fighting a losing action. Bells for the hours still kept. A lot of quiet expertise. Fear under obedience.

**Inhabitants.** Isarn, Durand, Odo when he is home, brothers, patients, the blind illuminator. Pilgrims at the shrine.

**Story use.** Reception. The petition. The warming relics. Isarn's wrong mercy (seal the cask, protect the name). Hub for Act II and the descent of Act IV.

---

## The infirmary

**Function.** Durand's ward. Act II boss room.

**History.** The first purpose of the house. Herb wine and pork fat, relics carried around the beds, the old remedies. Since the Eastern dust and the changed cask, some patients keep their limbs.

**Visual style.** Long room, small windows high up, a tau at the far end that patients see from their pillows. Linen. Spatulas. A cask on a trestle, stamped with the house mark, weeping at the seams a little. Men and women with charcoal arms, with new limbs that bend at untaught joints, with faces that still try to thank. Durand's clean wrappings.

**Feel.** Kindness as a smell — lard, wine, a brother's voice — over meat that belongs to the ward. Flies at the cask, leaving the wounds alone.

**Story use.** Harmful wine given as a remedy. Patients split in the fight, and protecting those who need help matters. When Durand falls, the wine beads like blood, then like sclerotia. Stopping its use brings real relief and allows the brothers to care for people safely.

---

## The bakehouse and the rye chapel

**Function.** Evidence of the contaminated food and the counterfeit's deceptive rites.

**History.** Clean wheat is reserved for the Mass. In the campaign's invented history, material from the Eastern box was mixed into ordinary bread as a supposed remedy. A side chapel became the setting for false rites around the box. These rites are not a valid Mass for another presence; the counterfeit exploits people's hopes and trust.

**Visual style.** Bakehouse: a black mouth of oven, peels, stamps in the tau, two bins — a nearly empty wheat, a full rye with spurred heads. Chapel: a portable altar, a crucifix that hangs a degree off true, crumbs in the cracks of the stone that move if stared at.

**Feel.** Heat and wrong bread. Odo can say the *Agnus Dei* in the main church. In the rye chapel he chokes.

**Story use.** Registers, grain bins, and witnesses reveal the route of harm. Prayer near the counterfeit exposes the presence hiding there. Removing contamination and making the ovens safe protects people. A crock of clean wheat can support restored worship without gating victory on Guarin's confession of a past atrocity.

---

## The scriptorium and the side chapel of the remnant

**Function.** Evidence. The ticking box.

**History.** The scriptorium in this fiction serves the shrine and charitable house. The side chapel was for lesser relics. Isarn accepted and honored the Eastern box without verifying its claim. This is his failure of discernment; holy provenance is not established by the donor's story.

**Visual style.** Scriptorium: one window, two desks, Anthony's temptations in progress, gold leaf hoarded. The marginal beasts match the Bourne road. The illuminator's eyes are milk. Side chapel: a locked grate, a small Eastern box of wood and silver gone dark, sitting on an altar-stone that has hairline cracks in a millstone pattern. It ticks, like a beetle. It is warm. Fire-scorch on the floor around it, a feeding.

**Feel.** Ink, metal-heat, a tick you feel in the teeth. The splinter can remain a reminder of the Cross as Guarin resists the box's claims.

**Story use.** Isarn's admission. Ordinary attempts to burn the box have failed. It must be broken on the terms Act IV reveals. Guarin's splinter is not a second key, and genuine devotion does not sustain the box.

---

## The maladrerie below the abbey

**Function.** Side dungeon. Bridge to Raimbaut.

**History.** Lepers kept downhill and downwind, as custom requires. A priest from the abbey says a Mass on Sundays from the other side of a ditch.

**Visual style.** Low huts, a ditch with lime, clappers hung on posts, white faces in doorways. A chapel the size of a shed, the Host passed on a long spoon.

**Feel.** Bell-clappers. Wind that stays in the dip. Shame that is social, until the blight uses it.

**Story use.** Talk of Raimbaut's commandery. Optional: a marked man who asks to be taken to Saillans.

# Act III — The Plague Lands

## The Isère and Rhône corridor

**Function.** Overworld of Act III. The blight as a system of roads.

**History.** Grain and pilgrims have always used this corridor. Romans-sur-Isère holds the collegiate church of Saint-Barnard. Valence is a bishopric Isarn would rather leave uninvolved. South and east, the Drôme and the rock of Crest.

**Visual style.** A wider, flatter light than the Royans. River, towpaths, burned granaries, abandoned castra on knuckles of rock. Rye in the bottoms, some fields cut in panic, some left standing and whispering. Charcoal camps in the oak woods, the stacks still smoking as if work continued. Processional litter: scourges, broken taus, a shoe filled with grain.

**Feel.** More sky, less safety. Heat. The *Te Deum* sometimes, without mouths. Distance. The sense that the valley was a room and this is the house.

**Story use.** Travel, ambushes, the enemy ladder stepping up. Early locust-things in iron scales at the inner edge.

---

## Abandoned castra and charcoal camps

**Function.** Side combat spaces. Predators, hostile creatures, and people who may still need help.

**History.** Petty towers emptied by the fire or by Lambert's drafts or by Raimbaut's recruiting. Charcoal burners kept working until the woods filled with pigs that had been at the rye.

**Visual style.** Open roofs, a hearth still warm, a rule-book of Raimbaut's in one tower (a copy, bad hand). Beehive charcoal stacks like black shrines. Men who are bandits, and men the blight has already titled.

**Feel.** Ash, oak-smoke, unburied food. Ordinary sin in the outer ring.

**Story use.** Outer-circle enemies. One camp has a portable altar where prayer and help from a priest can sustain the journey.

---

## Collegiate shadow of Romans

**Function.** Optional urban pause. A church still trying.

**History.** Saint-Barnard is a powerful collegiate church on the Isère. In 1101 it has restricted communion and is arguing with itself about rye.

**Visual style.** A larger Romanesque mass than Saint-Laurent. Canons in the cloister. A market that sells everything the ovens have run out of. Taus chalked and washed off.

**Feel.** Institutional noise — argument, bells, a town that believes walls are a theology.

**Story use.** A pause. A canon who offers spiritual counsel, prayer, and confession in their ordinary Christian place. Information on Crest and on the hospice where a chaplain of Raymond's host was seen.

---

## The commandery above the Drôme

**Function.** Raimbaut's house. Named dungeon.

**History.** A half-ruined castrum near Saillans, emptied in a local war a generation ago. Raimbaut has raised a white cloak over it and written a rule. His "brothers" are returned men: leprous, burned, sleepless, and a few uninfected postulants who wanted a purpose.

**Visual style.** Broken curtain wall patched with timber. A chapel painted with a Christ whose wounds have been gilded until they look like coins. White cloaks on a line, some with tau, some with a cross of Raimbaut's own drawing. Gloves nailed to a beam. Stored grain taken from whole-skinned neighbors, piled like a reliquary.

**Feel.** Barracks pride. Vinegar and illness. A bell rung for a chapter that is also a war-council. Courtesy at the gate.

**Story use.** Talk first. Fight. Spare the uninfected if the player will. The rule-book is evidence of a heresy of chivalry.

---

## The closed town of Crest

**Function.** Act III set-piece. Lambert's arithmetic.

**History.** A fortress-town on a rock above the Drôme, walls older than Lambert. When the fire began to look like administration, he left Valence, assumed a mitre Rome has yet to notice, and closed the gates. Then he built a second wall inside, and put the sick behind it.

**Visual style.** Rock, ochre stone, a keep that reads as a finger. Outer town too clean: swept, limewashed, a market of the healthy. Then the inner wall, newer, hurried, the mortar still pale. Behind it, a town in a town: shutters, taus, tainted bread handed out as a supposed holy remedy, plaster scratched *ex abysso locustae* in a hand foreign to the Drôme. Lambert's chapel is bare and correct. A ledger on the altar.

**Feel.** Outer: vinegar, order, the relief of walls. Inner: the Royans again, but denser, the sky cut off. The graffiti ticks like the box.

**Story use.** Audience with Lambert. The inner wall as dungeon. He can be killed or unseated before his chapter. The second is remembered. A child behind the inner wall may be Agnes if she was left on the road; otherwise it is someone else's child, which is the same arithmetic.

---

## The pilgrimage hospice

**Function.** Fulk's room. Act III climax.

**History.** An old hospice on the road that has always taken pilgrims going to Le Puy, to Rome, to the sea. In 1101 it takes anyone who can still walk and some who cannot. Fulk has been here, or in houses like it, since he left La-Motte.

**Visual style.** A long low room, pallets, a kitchen, a chapel with a decent crucifix and a Host Giraud would approve — wheat, on the days they have it. Fulk's pallet is in a corner he has made neat. Crumbs. A rag of Eastern cloth. Flies he leaves on his mouth. A small box, empty, lined with black dust: the memory of a reliquary.

**Feel.** Broth, unwashed wool, a politeness that sits oddly on the end of the world. When the voice is close, the smell of the blighted grain intrudes. Care among the pilgrims remains visible beside the fear.

**Story use.** Guarin protects Fulk during prayer and resistance to the binding; a priest of the hospice hears his confession. If the knot is cut, Fulk dies in grace here or on the road back. If killed with the knot tied, he hatches into a demon. Helping him turn toward Christ matters, without requiring Guarin to reveal a hidden atrocity.

# Act IV — The Pit in the Grain

The cathedral of the game is a descent. Start from the abbey the player already knows and take the floors out.

## The Antonine charnel

**Function.** First descent. The house's own dead, filed.

**History.** Limbs the fire took, boxed by year, because the brothers are hospitalers and because they believed the parts might be needed at the resurrection in recognizable form. 1095, 1096, 1097… 1101 still has room.

**Visual style.** A cellar off the infirmary, lime, shelves, tau-stamped boxes. Labels in Odo's hand and in worse hands. A 1099 box that ticks once and then keeps still. The smell of the ward with the wine stripped out.

**Feel.** Dust, lime, a register of losses. Odo, if present, can hardly stand.

**Story use.** Atmosphere and a key. Isarn's last letter may be here if he is already dead.

---

## The Merovingian crypt

**Function.** Older Christian dead. Proof the house was built on a house.

**History.** The shrine-church sits on an earlier burial ground. The brothers keep the crypt off the pilgrim circuit. Iron and lime. Names that have begun to forget they were Frankish.

**Visual style.** Rough vaults, sarcophagus-troughs, a faded chi-rho. Offerings from an older piety: a comb, a horse-bit, a grain-measure. Scratchings of 944 and 994 in a later hand — someone already counted soundings.

**Feel.** Colder than the charnel. A quiet older than the ward's. The first sense that the fire is older than the order.

**Story use.** The list of soundings. A seal that was tried and failed, Christian, local.

---

## The Roman granary and mithraeum

**Function.** Ancient religious remains and evidence of the blight's older local foothold.

**History.** This precise granary and mithraeum beneath the hospital are invented. Ancient remains give the descent depth without establishing a living medieval cult or making Christianity another version of that cult. Any older supernatural encounter belongs to the campaign's fictional history.

**Visual style.** Brick and opus reticulatum, amphora necks in the walls, a long dining-bench, a relief of the bull-killing with the head deliberately smashed — by bishops, or by something that wanted the field to itself. Grain-dust fused to the floor in spur shapes. A second relief, unfinished or defaced, of a figure with a millstone circlet.

**Feel.** Dry, for underground. Iron and old wheat. A banquet posture to the room.

**Story use.** Reveal evidence of the enemy's foothold and older attempts to contain it. Guarin can pray here and draw strength from Christ. Prayer does not lengthen the gauntlet or benefit the enemy.

---

## The older shaft

**Function.** Approach to the silo. Gauntlet of transfigured agents.

**History.** The granary was built against a shaft that predates it. The shaft uses the granary the way a hermit crab uses a shell. The cut of the stone keeps its own counsel.

**Visual style.** The masonry changes: a circular well lined with fused rye-glass, black and amber. Images in the glass if you move — Peter's corridor of fire; a sick companion on the road home; Fulk's false apparition; tainted provisions passing through Rivoire. The four agents wait on landings: Durand as a nurse of flies; Raimbaut as a leper-angel in a rule of iron; Lambert as a mitred locust counting; Fulk as the mouth if the knot held.

**Feel.** A draft upward, sweet. The enemy tries to make every step feel futile. Faith, allies, and the consequences of earlier acts of protection sustain Guarin.

**Story use.** Gauntlet. Evidence that earlier victories mattered and can help the present fight. Then the reliquary-heart: the Eastern box grown into a millstone-and-meat organ blocking the last drop.

# Act V — Apollyon

## The pit in the grain

**Function.** Final room. Unseating.

**History.** The office's local body. A silo.

**Visual style.** A black cylinder the size of a night sky. A floor you take on trust. Raining sclerotia that catch what light there is and look like a reverse snow. Abaddon crowned in the millstone, locust-knight-mill, the host around him as John wrote: human faces, women's hair, iron breastplates, scorpion tails. If there is architecture, it is ribs of a granary going up forever.

**Feel.** Quiet enough to hear a chronicler. Then the wings. Dust that is food and teeth. Abaddon's courtesy.

**Story use.** The last fight. Abaddon claims that the lives Guarin saves cannot matter and that evil will always win. Guarin knows otherwise through the goodness he has seen and the people he has helped. With Christ as his strength, he breaks the foothold and defeats the Destroyer. Prayer and blessed attacks can contribute to the final victory. The rain of sclerotia stops; the cylinder is a shaft again. Above, it is 1102, and the people of the valley can build a future.

# Memory rooms

These are remembered experiences of war and the journey home, or temptations through which the enemy renews Guarin's despair. They are not judgment-sights produced by unworthy communion. The same visual inspiration applies, with hotter light and too much gold, as if an illuminator had been believed.

## The floor of St. Peter's, Antioch

A dark cathedral, a hole in the flagstones, men passing a scrap of iron from hand to hand. Peter Bartholomew's face in torchlight. Adhemar on his feet. Hunger, fear, and hope among the men. This historical controversy does not establish a demonic rite.

## The sickroom on the road home

A pallet, a bowl of broth, a companion whose breath Guarin listens for. Exhaustion makes every mile seem pointless. Someone stays beside the sick man. As Guarin's hope returns, that care can take on a meaning he could not see before.

## Jerusalem, July 1099

The violence and losses Guarin witnessed, and the faith and hopes with which he went east. The splinter is bought here. Its market provenance does not make his devotion a hidden key for evil. The scene contains no imposed personal-atrocity confession.

## Peter's corridor of fire

Good Friday, 1099. Two piles of wood. A man in a tunic carrying the claimed lance. Fulk's face in the crowd. This is a remembered ordeal and controversy, not proof that blessing empowers the Destroyer.
