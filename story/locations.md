# Dias Irae — Locations

Active rooms and routes for autumn **1149**, in [England](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=England), around [Lewes](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Lewes%2C+East+Sussex%2C+United+Kingdom). Plot: [story-arch.md](story-arch.md). People: [characters.md](characters.md). Reconstruction limits: [place and material culture](../docs/research/1149-england/place-and-material-culture.md).

**Rookmere**, its parish, the care house, the veterans' refuge, Hamon's enclosure, and Blackthorn Hold are invented. The historical town and its religious community supply the wider setting. The playable circuit compresses travel and does not identify a recovered medieval manor, exact mill site, or modern road as an authentic 1149 route.

---

## The land in one look

A worked countryside with stubble, gardens, grazing, timber, plaster, selected masonry, and paths that become difficult in wet weather. The town brings markets, religious neighbors, administration, and strangers. Shortening light and dependence on stores give the autumn its pressure.

Ordinary life remains visible. Someone repairs a wheel, a patient helps another reach a stool, a widow shares food, and a household keeps worship. The invented blight appears along one compromised provision route; it does not turn every English field or every sick person into the enemy.

The palette remains poor and worn: wet earth, grain-gold, soot, limewash, faded cloth, dull iron, and linen darkened through use. The Antonine tau and pig imagery no longer organize the whole region. The care house uses ordinary clothing and simple devotional signs. The veterans' badge is an invention.

The required rain at the cross is scene weather. No specific 1149 storm, famine, flood, or eclipse has been reconstructed. The final impossible depth is supernatural fiction, not local archaeology.

# Act I — The House Without Protection

## The last road to Rookmere

**Function.** Arrival and movement tutorial; the first ordinary people and signs of wrong.

**History.** Invented local road Robert left in 1147. A short playable approach stands for a longer journey home. No real surviving cross or modern route is assigned his biography.

**Visual style.** Cut fields, grazing, hedges where appropriate, a damaged cart, an abandoned relief sack. The black rye appears in a particular sack or store; the wider harvest is not all the same crop. Robert's borrowed mule hesitates at a movement in the ditch.

**Feel.** Wind, work noises, then a gap in them. Damp wool and an ordinary farm smell interrupted by sweetness from the suspect grain.

**Story use.** A person who refuses the relief bread gives the first usable clue. Help can be offered before the first fight.

## The stone cross

**Function.** Required early prayer scene.

**History.** A familiar local cross in the invented parish landscape, known to Robert before departure. Its position and construction are fiction.

**Visual style.** Worn stone, wet grass, mud at his knees, helmet beside him. His hand rests against the cross while his shoulders shake.

**Feel.** Rain, uneven breathing, and enough silence for him to finish speaking.

**Story use.** The exact prayer in the outline. Robert asks Christ for hope, will, guidance, and strength. The scene's answer unfolds through the campaign; it is not reduced to an instant statistical refill.

## Rookmere hall and yard

**Function.** Household hub and refuge.

**History.** An invented subordinate manor in the Warenne sphere. Its modest hall and service buildings are not the historical earl's castle. Walter died in spring 1149; Alice has kept the stores, obligations, and neighbors in view.

**Visual style.** Smoke-darkened beams, repaired plaster, shutters, a stable, a kitchen, bedding moved to make room. Godric's key belt. Separate stores with Alice's practical marks. Robert's old place still has a peg.

**Feel.** A house whose people have continued useful work while afraid. Alice can make someone move a bench without raising her voice.

**Inhabitants.** Alice, Godric, household workers, Edith when sheltered, and neighbors given room.

**Story use.** Copies and recollection of Walter's acts expose misuse of the household seal. Return visits show the effects of safe supplies, rescued people, and repairs. The home should visibly improve through particular interventions.

## Parish of Saint Peter

**Function.** Worship, spiritual support, sanctuary, and Hugh's later release.

**History.** Fictional local parish. Father Anselm serves it; Walter has one ordinary grave in its yard. The site is not a relocated French shrine or a claim about an existing church's precise 1149 state.

**Visual style.** A modest painted church, worn sacred images, a font, a small sacristy, and a yard with room for neighbors keeping vigil. It need not resemble a complete later Gothic building. The crucifix remains a place of genuine devotion.

**Feel.** Familiar prayer heard through exhaustion. People still come for one another.

**Story use.** Anselm keeps worship and care available. Later he hears Hugh's confession while Robert protects the approach. Attacks come from the binding and hostile creatures, not from the sacrament.

## Rookmere mill

**Function.** Work site, supply evidence, and Edith's family stake.

**History.** Invented working watermill serving the holding. Hamon's men obstruct access and take the miller after he challenges a collection. A specific historical monopoly, toll, or mill location is not asserted.

**Visual style.** Wet timber, stone, meal dust, sacks with ordinary wear, and a dark compromised lot set apart from clean grain. A sweep-brush and the miller's tools make the missing person tangible.

**Feel.** Water and the wheel when it can move; an unnatural knocking from one sack when it cannot.

**Story use.** Edith identifies the delivery. Restoring access lets the mill help the community again. The building is not obligatorily burned by its priest, and recovery does not require making all grain safe by sight alone.

## The collection yard

**Function.** Ernald's first named confrontation.

**History.** Fictional enclosure beside the manor's stores where collectors can hold a cart and threaten dependants. Its exact levy and claimed mandate are invented.

**Visual style.** Grain still being lifted into a cart, an armed man holding a family away from the gate, a horse ready to carry the collection onward. Clear paths let Robert protect someone from an attacker.

**Feel.** An ordinary argument turning violent. The first inhuman movement comes from the suspect sack, after the human threat is understood.

**Story use.** Stop Ernald and active attackers, secure surrender if possible, and protect the grain and people. Godric remains an ally; sickness is not the targeting rule.

# Act II — The Bread of Mercy

## Farm road, crossing, and storehouse

**Function.** Escort route; practical links between home, care, and trade.

**History.** Invented compressed route through the district. No exact bridge, ferry right, or road alignment is assigned without further research.

**Visual style.** Fields after harvest, grazing, a stream crossing, patched fencing, and a storehouse whose roof has been partly repaired. Clean supplies travel separately from the compromised distribution.

**Feel.** Wet paths, the need to keep a cart moving, and people waiting to decide whether they can trust its escort.

**Story use.** Help travelers, stop predatory collectors and hostile creatures, and keep the usable route open. The scene does not require a later uniformed public flagellant movement.

## House of Saint Michael

**Function.** Act II hub: care, inquiry, error admitted, and defense.

**History.** Explicitly fictional charitable household supported by local gifts and religious neighbors. It is distinct from the historical town's Cluniac priory and is not presented as a documented Antonine or military-order foundation.

**Visual style.** A small chapel, kitchen, a few ward rooms, straw bedding, washed linen, a yard, and space for stores. Ordinary work clothes. Saint Michael's devotional imagery can connect protection with the house's vocation; its particular image is an art choice.

**Feel.** Broth, wet wool, patient work, and anxiety over the next meal. Quiet expertise matters more than an elaborate institutional uniform.

**Inhabitants.** Thomas, Wulfstan, Oswin, Agatha, patients, and helpers.

**Story use.** Thomas's admission and Wulfstan's change of course. A place the player helps preserve, with real Christian service continuing after the wrong is exposed.

## Ward and kitchen

**Function.** Patients to protect, suspect supplies stopped, and the seizure-party set-piece.

**History.** Fictional care arrangements; no exact historical bed count or treatment protocol is reconstructed. Harm followed particular relief deliveries.

**Visual style.** Beds, stools, a washing basin, food being carried, and room to move patients away from a threat. A suspect bin is closed and separated; replacement meals come from a different store.

**Feel.** An ordinary act of care interrupted by armed men. A frightened patient can still help another.

**Story use.** Wulfstan and Agatha organize movement while Robert stops attackers. The hostile ward intruder wears a collector's outline; it is not a nurse or patient's illness transformed into guilt. Wulfstan is not a compulsory boss.

## Record room and empty casket recess

**Function.** Evidence of a particular supply route and the counterfeit's removal.

**History.** The house keeps modest written records in this fiction. Oswin has copied useful entries. Thomas accepted Hugh's gift, delayed warning others, and later lost the remaining lot and casket to Hamon.

**Visual style.** A small desk, parchment, wax, an ordinary seal, names and deliveries, and a recess scorched around a now-empty shape. No standardized printed forms or modern medical charts.

**Feel.** Paper that matters because someone may be taken away. The absence of the casket is more unsettling than an ornate display.

**Story use.** Match accounts and witness recollection. Preserve a copy. Identify what went to Blackthorn Hold without requiring one skill roll or one threatened book as the sole route forward.

# Act III — Whose Peace?

## The market neighborhood

**Function.** Wider human stakes, exchange, witnesses, and allies.

**History.** The historical town supplies the setting; the campaign's particular market encounters and local crisis are invented. The historical castle and Cluniac community remain separate institutions with their own authority. Robert does not acquire them.

**Visual style.** Useful work, sellers, carts, familiar and unfamiliar faces, worship, and a clerk trying to hear someone over noise. Keep the complete later defenses and church fabric out of an unverified reconstruction.

**Feel.** Life continuing around fear. Trade, argument, a meal shared, and a person choosing to stay with a stranger.

**Story use.** Find someone able to assess a written claim, gain protection for witnesses, and obtain clean supplies. The whole town is not the hostile faction.

## Hamon's enclosure

**Function.** Captives, the supply monopoly, and confrontation with abused authority.

**History.** Invented guarded yard and storehouse near the market route. It is not a recorded seizure of the town or a bishopric. Hamon's claimed commission and exact demands are fictional.

**Visual style.** A gate, grain behind bars, detained people, copies of commands, and the seal case kept well away from the dirty sacks. Release paths matter as much as the champion's fighting space.

**Feel.** An orderly explanation that does not answer a hungry person's question.

**Story use.** Free the miller and other captives; expose the collection; stop Hamon and his active enforcers. Witnesses and an allied household can sustain the change. No modern trial system or royal appointment power is implied.

## Gilbert's refuge

**Function.** An existing knightly ideal appropriated into a predatory private rule.

**History.** Invented veterans' yard and repaired fortified dwelling. The Brotherhood of the Wounded has no verified affiliation with a real military order.

**Visual style.** Patched pale cloaks, an irregular invented badge, a rule read aloud, repaired arms, and stores taken from neighbors. A chapel can still contain a proper crucifix; the wrong is what Gilbert requires people to do.

**Feel.** Barracks courtesy, belonging, and the fear of losing that belonging. Some men still want to protect others.

**Story use.** Hear Gilbert, show predation, help coerced men leave, and stop his attacks. Those who reject his commands can later guard the clean-supply convoy. Disease and scars do not determine membership in the enemy group.

## Return to Saint Peter's

**Function.** Hugh's release under attack.

**History.** Same fictional parish already known from Act I. Robert brings Hugh here because he can trust its priest and community.

**Visual style.** A pallet near the church, Anselm beside Hugh, Robert at the approach, neighbors helping keep frightened people away from the fight. The binding's movement is distinct from the care being offered.

**Feel.** A voice struggling to belong to its speaker, ordinary prayer, and the noise of attacks outside.

**Story use.** Protect Hugh's chance to reject the false master. Anselm hears confession; Robert fights. Hugh can survive and recover. If he is lost, copies and other witnesses still lead toward the hold.

# Act IV — The Hold and the Pit

## Blackthorn Hold: outer yard and depot

**Function.** End the guarded distribution and protect those still detained.

**History.** Fictional damaged fortification reused by Hamon's network during recent political uncertainty. Its builder, damage, license, and tenure are invention. No real site is assigned a secret demonic history.

**Visual style.** Earthwork, a patched palisade, selected masonry, rooms used as stores, stolen sacks, carts, and hurried confinement. The chapel houses the casket that Hamon claims will keep his distribution safe.

**Feel.** Human acts remain legible before the impossible architecture begins. Someone built the bars and kept the keys.

**Story use.** Free captives, stop remaining active guards and distribution, and secure a route for allies. Alice, Godric, caregivers, and released veterans can help above ground while Robert descends.

## Chapel, store pits, and well passage

**Function.** Trace the counterfeit's influence into the foothold.

**History.** Recent store pits and reused rooms are fictional local construction. The lower well passage becomes supernatural depth; it is not a transported Roman granary, Mithraic sanctuary, or French crypt.

**Visual style.** Worn devotional painting, the casket in a falsely honored place, dust on sacks, broken storage timbers, and a well whose depth no longer fits the hill above it. The change into impossible space must be readable.

**Feel.** A grinding below the floor that continues when the mill above is still.

**Story use.** The inquiry's records, Hugh's account, and the casket's effects converge. Prayer and ordinary evidence serve distinct parts of discernment. Genuine sacred objects are not the engine of the corruption.

## The shaft and casket-heart

**Function.** Act IV gauntlet and the fight that breaks the counterfeit.

**History.** Explicit supernatural fiction. No list of fabricated historical excavations or earlier pagan survivals is required.

**Visual style.** Grain dust fused into black surfaces, iron like bindings around stores, spaces that remember a march and an enclosure at once. The casket grows into the depot's wrong heart.

**Feel.** The enemy's argument made physical: every passage seems to return to another locked door and another hungry person.

**Story use.** Robert perseveres with help and the evidence of earlier victories. Creatures of confinement, hunger, and false promises oppose him. Rescued people do not automatically return as compulsory undead bosses. Breaking the heart opens the final approach.

# Act V — Apollyon

## The impossible granary

**Function.** Final confrontation and defeat of the local foothold.

**History.** Fictional space beyond the ordinary depot, shaped by the Destroyer's presence.

**Visual style.** A black granary larger than the hold, dust falling like reverse snow, and a floor made uncertain by the movement beneath it. Abaddon combines the locust host's human face, hair, iron breastplates, and scorpion sting with a ruler's circlet and a millstone.

**Feel.** Enough quiet to hear his courteous claim that every rescue is futile; then wings and grinding.

**Story use.** Robert knows the people and acts that contradict the claim. He fights through Christ with the will and conviction for which he prayed. Prayer and blessed attacks can help defeat the enemy. The impossible granary contracts to a broken shaft.

## The district after the fight

**Function.** Epilogue in **1150**; the prayer's fulfillment made visible.

**History.** The local recovery is fiction. Stephen's reign and the disputed succession continue beyond this victory.

**Visual style.** The manor's table has people at it. The mill works. Seed is kept for planting. A ward has clean meals and someone keeping vigil. The parish continues worship. Injuries and losses remain, with useful work beside them.

**Feel.** Relief with things still to do. Ordinary voices are audible again.

**Story use.** Alice, Edith, Hugh if protected, and others have futures shaped by what happened. Robert recognizes their goodness and knows the struggle mattered. His local victory is real even while the wider historical conflict continues.

# Memory rooms

These are authored experiences from the Second Crusade and the return, not firsthand memories of the First Crusade or supernatural proof of a historian's accusation.

## The broken march, 1147–1148

A difficult mountain passage, lost animals, scattered supplies, and men trying to keep a companion moving. Specific tactics and terrain need reference selection before production. The memory can contain both fear and an overlooked act of care.

## The failed siege, July 1148

The expedition against [Damascus](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Damascus%2C+Syria): orchards, thirst, uncertain commands, withdrawal, and distrust. Robert's particular experience is fiction. The scene does not turn every later bribery accusation into verified fact.

## Pilgrimage, 1148

[Jerusalem](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Jerusalem), a prayer kept despite exhaustion, and the splinter bought on a claim of holy provenance. This is not the city's conquest in 1099. Devotion remains sincere while an item's claim remains uncertain.

## The sickroom on the way west, 1148–1149

A pallet, a bowl, a companion's breathing, and someone who stayed beside him. As Robert's hope returns, he recognizes the goodness that despair made difficult to see.

Memory encounters contain no compulsory personal-atrocity confession, cannibalism mechanic, or rule that genuine worship feeds the enemy.
