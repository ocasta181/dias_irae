# Reconsidering the campaign for autumn 1149

This is the active authorial reconsideration of *Dias Irae*. The homecoming is after the Second Crusade, in [England](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=England), around [Lewes](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Lewes%2C+East+Sussex%2C+United+Kingdom). Rookmere and the campaign's particular incidents are invented. The revised story documents own the resulting plot, cast, map, and systems proposals.

The research foundation is [chronology and politics](chronology-and-politics.md), [orders, religion, and care](orders-and-religion.md), and [place and material culture](place-and-material-culture.md). These sources establish the surrounding world; they do not make the fictional events historical.

## The question the new setting puts to the knight

The knight returns from a failed eastern expedition to a home where protection is sold, charity is obstructed, and allegiance is used to excuse private harm. His fear is that vows, sacrifice, and courage accomplish nothing. His vocation becomes concrete: keep people alive, tell the truth about a wrong, oppose the men exploiting them, and defeat the supernatural enemy that insists no good can endure.

The historical earl's death on the same crusade connects distant war to ordinary household uncertainty. The wider royal conflict creates pressure without becoming the player’s problem to solve. The campaign's victory is the survival and renewal of these people in **1150**, while the historical succession remains unsettled.

The exact prayer and Christ's real answer remain the governing arc. Neither the chosen English district nor military-order availability requires making the protagonist a professed monk-knight. He remains a secular knight who can return to kin, inherit household duties, and act with allies.

## The new premise

**Robert of Rookmere** comes home in autumn 1149. His sister **Alice** has kept the manor and its neighbors alive, but **Hamon**, a fictional estate agent, has taken control of a local provision route. He demands grain and service in return for a protection he does not provide. Food reaching the poor through this route brings a burning illness and impossible creatures.

**Father Hugh**, Robert's former company chaplain, brought an Eastern casket and a small lot of rye home earlier that year. A deceptive apparition had promised that the casket would preserve people from hunger and death. Hugh gave it to the **House of Saint Michael**, an invented care household. Its head, **Master Thomas**, trusted a frightened donor and delayed acknowledging the harm. When the house questioned the provisions, Hamon seized the suspect lot, took the casket, and made them part of his controlled relief supply.

The casket promises merciful protection but establishes Abaddon's local foothold. Robert's investigation must interrupt both the physical route of harm and the coercion keeping people dependent on it. Safe food, truthful testimony, care, prayer, and righteous force serve the same struggle.

## Place and authority changes

| Earlier organizing element | Active decision |
|---|---|
| French petty castellany under Albon | Invented Rookmere manor in the Warenne sphere; household protection and disputed demands. |
| Antonine abbey-hospital | Invented House of Saint Michael with local religious and lay support; the real Cluniac community remains a separate neighbor. |
| Gorge-and-pilgrimage circuit | Home parish, mill and crossing, care house, market and records, veterans' refuge, fortified provision depot. |
| Sealed town governed by a rogue bishop | A coercive enclosure and grain monopoly run by a lay agent within the fictional local disturbance. |
| Uniformed flagellant band | Removed; frightened neighbors and predatory men do not require a later public movement. |
| French charnel, Merovingian crypt, Roman mithraeum | Replaced by recent store pits and damaged local fortification, followed by explicitly impossible supernatural depth. |

This location changes the causes of vulnerability. A dead lord, an heir's interests, claimed commissions, local obligations, and withheld provisions are the plot's human machinery. None requires making the real historical family secretly demonic.

## Five acts reconsidered

| Act | Immediate struggle | What becomes known | Durable good |
|---|---|---|---|
| I — The House Without Protection | Robert reaches home, prays, and stops Ernald's armed collection. | Illness clusters around particular relief supplies; Alice has kept separate stores. | Neighbors and seed grain are protected; the manor becomes a working refuge. |
| II — The Bread of Mercy | Escort supplies and defend the care house against seizure. | Hugh's casket arrived before the illness; Thomas delayed admitting a problem; Hamon removed it. | Suspect food is stopped, safe meals resume, and caregivers choose truth. |
| III — Whose Peace? | Challenge Hamon's claims and Gilbert's predatory protection. | Witnesses and records expose the route; Hugh's testimony identifies the false apparition. | Captives are freed, coerced veterans can leave, and Hugh can turn toward Christ. |
| IV — The Hold and the Pit | Allies secure the living supply route while Robert enters Blackthorn Hold. | The casket and guarded depot sustain the supernatural foothold. | Earlier help becomes reciprocal; people keep working while the knight descends. |
| V — Apollyon | Break the counterfeit and defeat the Destroyer through Christ. | Abaddon's claim that every rescue is futile is confronted by lived evidence. | The local grip breaks; a community has food, worship, work, and a future in 1150. |

The old obligatory deaths, transfigured-boss procession, institutional medical spectacle, and saintly-history flashbacks do not choose the new encounters. Hamon and Gilbert are responsible for their acts. Wulfstan, Godric, and Hugh now have opportunities to become evidence of goodness and recovery instead of predetermined corpses.

## Themes supported by this date

- **Vows and protection.** A promise made before God is tested in what its bearer does for a frightened neighbor.
- **Authority and responsibility.** A seal, command, rank, or royal allegiance does not make exploitation righteous. Legitimate obligations and people who keep them remain visible.
- **Charity under pressure.** Sharing clean food, keeping a bed, guarding a witness, and admitting a harmful mistake change the world the player sees.
- **Failure and hope.** The failed eastern expedition explains Robert's exhaustion. Christ restores the will and conviction to act, and the local victory gives that action lasting meaning.
- **Belonging.** Household French, local English, clerical Latin, veterans, widows, tenants, and travelers meet through particular relationships rather than fixed national moral camps.

The Chronicle and Bernard are historical comparisons, not dialogue scripts supposedly circulating locally in this exact season. No political faction, religion, or illness becomes the supernatural cause by category.

## Production decisions and evidence limits

This is a coherent working revision authorized by the author, not a report of actual events. Numerical progression, exact encounter scripting, final art, and terrain measurements remain design work. The historical prior, exact local levy, Isabel's first-marriage date, medical procedures, complete buildings, and specific weather or sky events retain their research limits.

Earlier art records keep their source names, image IDs, preferences, and generation provenance. They are reference material for a revised cast, not proof that an old named figure or costume remains canon. The active story and production-facing prose should use the new names when those documents are next revised.
