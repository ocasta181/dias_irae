# English town settings for Guarin's story

**Recommendation:** if an English setting is chosen, prefer **[Lewes](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Lewes%2C+East+Sussex%2C+England) and a small fictional manor in its surrounding district**. **[Castle Acre](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Castle+Acre%2C+Norfolk%2C+England)** is strongest for a more intimate campaign. **[Shrewsbury](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Shrewsbury%2C+Shropshire%2C+England)** is the third finalist when pressure from armed local power should be more prominent. The working setting and late-summer-to-winter **1101** date remain unchanged.

The ranking serves Guarin's [Christian arc](../../../story/story-arch.md): a wounded returning crusader sincerely asks Jesus for strength of will and conviction; Christ answers; the people he protects, the goodness he encounters, and his meaningful victory restore hope. A setting must give him a community to love and practical opportunities to save it. Narrative fit determines rank. Historical dates identify current leadership and available institutions.

Evidence reviewed on **5–6 October 2026**.

## Ranked decision

An invented manor outside a real center lets Guarin know his parish without making him the historical owner of a great lord's castle. An **honour** was a lord's collection of estates and rights, often scattered; it is not one compact territory belonging to the protagonist.

## What the working date gives the story

**1101 gives Guarin a recent victorious crusade and a troubled homecoming.** Duke Robert Curthose returned from the armed pilgrimage and challenged his brother Henry I's kingship that summer. The Worcester chronicle describes negotiation and settlement rather than a battle between their assembled armies. A knight can therefore return to political uncertainty without finding the whole country in civil war. Local predation, disputed obligations, illness, and poverty can be the campaign's particular crisis. [Worcester chronicle, 1100–1101 annals, 1854 translation](https://www.melocki.org.uk/worcester/Chronicle.html).

Service in Robert's crusading host is a useful **story option** for a household with continental connections. Guarin's ancestry, companions, and homeward journey remain invention. Review the existing southern host and war memories if relocation is chosen; they do not automatically become local English history.

The conquest is only thirty-five years old. Older people can remember changed landholding, lost houses, new patrons, and earlier churches. This supplies believable disagreement within a community. It does not require Guarin's spiritual arc to become an investigation of his family's guilt. English and French speakers can cooperate, resent one another, and show kindness across household boundaries. Latin connects educated clergy, worship, and written records. The [culture report](culture.md) provides the broader language evidence; it does not establish every candidate's local literacy or each character's fluency.

**Domesday is earlier evidence.** Its 1086 entries establish settlement, resources, and previous ownership, not exact autumn-1101 possession or a complete population count. Survey entries retain their date; later succession and forfeiture require separate evidence.

## 1. Lewes: the strongest district for a community worth saving

**Why it serves the arc.** Guarin can return to an invented nearby manor with kin, tenants, a familiar priest, and people who knew him before he went east. The town adds tradespeople, servants, the poor, travelers, and religious neighbors. His interventions preserve lives; later help can come from those same people. The ending can show ordinary work and worship returning across connected communities.

**Historical support.** Lewes was already a significant borough in 1086: the survey data record **127 burgesses** and royal and Warenne interests. This supports a town rather than an isolated castle settlement, without fixing its 1101 population or rights. The castle is a post-Conquest Warenne foundation. William II de Warenne succeeded in 1088 and supported Curthose in 1101; his forfeiture and subsequent restoration make uninterrupted late-1101 possession unsafe to assume. The exact transition should be checked if it drives the plot. [Open Domesday, Lewes, Sussex 12,1](https://opendomesday.org/place/TQ4110/lewes/); [Castle Studies Group, Lewes catalogue, pp. 72–73](https://castlestudiesgroup.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Shell-Keeps-Catalogue1-Lewes-low-res-02-09.pdf); [retrospective biography of William II](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography,_1885-1900/Warenne,_William_de_(d.1138)).

The Cluniac priory dedicated to Saint Pancras was founded in **1078–1082**, on an earlier religious site. Prior Lanzo led the first monks. A living religious community is therefore secure before Guarin's homecoming, with both local roots and connections beyond the district. The historical community should contain sincere worship and service alongside any invented frightened or corrupt office-holder. [Lewes Priory Trust, foundation history](https://www.lewespriory.org.uk/history-overview); [the first community](https://www.lewespriory.org.uk/the-beginning).

The Trust says the hospital dedicated to Saint Nicholas was reportedly founded about **1085**, outside the western town boundary, for infirm residents, pilgrims, and the sick. Its wording is explicitly traditional. The linked archival study could not be retrieved, so this report does **not** independently establish that foundation date. A small fictional care household is sufficient if hospital care remains important. [Trust's hospital account, reviewed in the browser](https://www.lewespriory.org.uk/hospital-of-st-nicholas).

**Quest geography — story inference.** Use the river corridor and adjacent hill country to connect the home parish, town market, priory, rural estates, and a woodland refuge. A mill and its grain suppliers can form one practical problem; withheld food, abusive demands, an armed household, or a counterfeit remedy can form others. Exact mill sites, transport capacity, crop proportions, and travel times remain to be selected. Modern roads and present river engineering must not supply the medieval route automatically.

**Agency and invention.** Guarin can protect dependants, escort someone, confront a predatory officer, and win religious allies. Broader authority needs an intelligible local dispute. An invented steward can misuse uncertainty around his lord while other agents keep faith with their duties. The historical earl can remain offstage.

## 2. Castle Acre: the most intimate hometown campaign

**Why it serves the arc.** Castle Acre makes saving these particular people matter. Parish, lord's administration, monks, and farming households repeatedly cross Guarin's path. A servant sharing a meal, a monk remaining with a patient, and a neighbor returning to help become recognizable people. Return visits can show lasting consequences within a small district.

**Historical support.** Acre already had a settlement, church, and an important landholder called Toki before 1066. Post-Conquest changes brought the holding to William I de Warenne around **1070**. His castle acted as residence and administrative center for his regional estates; his son succeeded in **1088**. This offers local memory of both continuity and displacement. The family leader's 1101 political uncertainty is the same Warenne problem described for Lewes, not a separate secure ownership story. [English Heritage, early settlement and lordship](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/castle-acre-castle-and-bailey-gate/history/).

A small Cluniac community arrived from Lewes between **1081 and 1085**. Its established presence supports an actual Christian neighbor to the castle administration. A religious house can receive travelers and provide a plausible setting for care, prayer, records, and disagreements about obligations. Its precise 1101 prior, staffing, beds for outsiders, and medical practice were not established here. [English Heritage, foundation and early development](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/castle-acre-castle-acre-priory/history/).

**Quest geography — story inference.** A short river-valley circuit can contain the home parish, a mill, fields, a crossing, the religious house, and an outlying holding occupied by armed men. Grain and dues provide plausible reasons for people to move between them. Neither a documented local ergot epidemic nor the location of a specific working 1101 mill has been established. The campaign can instead begin with extortion, a frightened household, illness requiring care, or several connected wrongs.

**Agency and invention.** Guarin should hold an invented subordinate manor or serve in a local household. Calling the real Warenne castle his inherited petty castellany would change its historical ownership and scale. With that boundary, he can be the man who knows the lanes, tenants, and obligations and can organize protection when an official fails them. The monastery need not be the source of the evil: it can provide the courage, truthful witness, and charity that let his resistance succeed.

**Tradeoff.** Fewer urban institutions favor an intimate campaign. Any expansion should follow the needs of its people, rather than reproduce every institution in the previous map.

## 3. Shrewsbury: strength to confront coercive power

**Why it serves the arc.** Soldiers, demands on provisions, and protected officials can make Guarin feel insufficient. Christian courage and cooperation then give him allies and real agency. A family manor outside the town preserves the personal hometown; the town concentrates stronger authority.

**Historical support.** The castle controlled the neck of a river loop containing the pre-Conquest borough. It existed by **1069**; the survey records the loss of **51 tax-paying households** to its construction. That is a specific local conquest memory, not evidence that the town remained ruined in 1101. Robert of Bellême is the current earl in the Worcester chronicle; his rebellion and loss of English power belong to **1102**, after the campaign's main season. [Nigel Baker, excavation report, January 2020, pp. 2–4](https://castlestudiestrust.org/docs/SHREWSBURY-CASTLE-19-REPORT-1-20.pdf); [Worcester chronicle, 1101–1102 annals](https://www.melocki.org.uk/worcester/Chronicle.html).

Roger de Montgomery founded the Benedictine abbey in **1083**, on the site of an existing English church. Its presence gives religious life a history beyond the current earl's politics. The abbey can be an ally without portraying its founder or every monk as faultless. Its keeper's foundation account does not establish an external hospital ward, its 1101 capacity, or the historical conduct of a particular infirmarian. [Shrewsbury Abbey, foundation account](https://www.shrewsburyabbey.com/).

**Quest geography — story inference.** The loop, castle approach, crossing, abbey neighborhood, farms, and nearby wooded country create connected but distinct spaces. Control of an approach or transport of provisions can matter to the household and town together. Welsh-speaking neighbors or travelers are credible regional possibilities, but actual communities, names, and loyalties require local work. The frontier should contain ordinary families and exchange as well as armed conflict.

**Agency and invention.** Make the immediate antagonists local officers, a predatory armed group, or an estate holder whose conduct Guarin can stop with help. He cannot simply depose the historical earl because the player completed a dungeon. A priest who testifies, townspeople who refuse a demand, or an officer who finally acts rightly can turn the balance. Their courage fulfills the story's claim that goodness remains in fellow men.

**Tradeoff.** Royal war and frontier conquest can dominate. Keep room for the separately dated 1102 events. Hostile portraits of Bellême record accusations and judgments, not proven demon worship.

## 4. Rochester: credible care and a useful division of authority

**Why it serves the arc.** Care for vulnerable people can show Guarin a world worth saving: established charity, a household struggling to help, and people opposing an abuse. Sickness creates a need for protection, not an enemy identity.

**Historical support.** Rochester combined a bishopric, an old walled settlement, a major river crossing, and royal castle interests. The rebellion and siege of **1088** are recent local history. Bishop **Gundulf, 1077–1108**, rebuilt castle defenses for the king and supported religious foundations; secular and ecclesiastical claims should therefore remain distinct. He is a living historical leader, not an empty office for an invented irregular bishop to assume. [English Heritage, early castle and crossing](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/rochester-castle/history/); [cathedral's Gundulf biography](https://www.rochestercathedral.org/forc/bishopgundulf).

Historic England dates the hospital dedicated to Saint Bartholomew to **1078**. The cathedral biography associates it with care for people described as lepers. That supplies stronger pre-1101 foundation evidence than the traditional early date for Lewes's hospital. It does not establish the hospital's exact early layout, routines, or ability to absorb the campaign's victims. [Historic England, list entry 1268238, Details and History](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1268238); [Gundulf's foundations](https://www.rochestercathedral.org/forc/bishopgundulf).

**Quest geography — story inference.** Put Guarin's invented manor near a smaller watercourse feeding the main river. Connect parish care, rural supplies, the crossing, the hospital outside the town, and an estate whose armed men obstruct help. The road and river can carry news and goods without requiring a large maritime campaign. Existing Roman history can provide archaeological depth; a precise underground granary, cult room, or demonic prison remains invention.

**Agency and tradeoff.** Guarin can protect patients, secure provisions, and expose dishonest officers with allies' help. Gundulf can remain a distant legitimate bishop, with fictional clergy divided over a local wrong. Making the historical bishop a demonic tyrant would be additional invention. Choose Rochester for these relationships; its early hospital alone does not decide the setting.

## 5. Richmond: a concentrated lordship and a protective vocation

**Why it serves the arc.** A small household in Richmond's castle district can be home, with the settlement and religious community widening Guarin's circle. Local obligations and officials can be formidable without making him responsible for the whole north.

**Historical support.** The castle belongs to the post-Conquest establishment of Alan Rufus's honour, probably from the late **1070s**. Domesday describes a **castlery**, an estate organized to support a castle. The keeper's succession account moves from Alan's death in **1093** through Alan Niger to Stephen, but does not give a precise Stephen handover date. Do not obtain an exact 1101 resident lord from that compressed sequence alone. Secure borough privileges are documented by **1145**; they should not be treated as a charter already issued in 1101. [English Heritage, foundation and succession](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/richmond-castle/history-and-stories/history/).

Historic England dates the small Benedictine priory dedicated to Saint Martin to **1100**, as a dependent cell. Its proximity supplies a credible local religious community; its later estate holdings and recorded complement do not establish every detail of the first year's operation. [Historic England, scheduled monument 1012995, Details](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1012995).

Saint Martin's older cloak story is a useful **literary association** with the Sword and Mantle prayer: Sulpicius Severus describes a soldier using his sword to divide his cloak for a destitute man, then seeing Christ clothed in it. This is an older Christian devotional text, not evidence for a specific 1101 sermon or a requirement that Guarin copy Martin's entire life. [*Life of Saint Martin*, chapter 3](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3501.htm).

**Quest geography and invention.** A valley circuit can join parish, castle settlement, religious house, river terrace, farms, and more exposed upland routes. Choose the actual medieval crossing and mill evidence before fixing them on a map. Guarin can oppose coercion in a nearby holding and receive help from villagers and monks. The more mountainous surroundings offer atmosphere, but they are secondary to the people and relationships. A large independent hospital, a named 1101 prior, and exact household jurisdiction remain unverified.

## 6. Evesham: goodness expressed through ordinary service

**Why it serves the arc.** Evesham emphasizes practical charity, food, labor, and the use of wealth. Guarin can know neighbors and religious servants. Stopping an abusive demand has a visible consequence: someone eats, a family remains together, and work becomes possible again.

**Historical support.** The 1086 survey records abbey lordship, **27 smallholders**, plough teams, meadow, and a mill at Evesham. These are a dated small-settlement and resource picture, not the town's complete 1101 population. Other abbey estates combined mills, fisheries, woodland, and salt rights, making institutional wealth part of a wider production network. [Open Domesday, Worcestershire 10,1](https://opendomesday.org/place/SP0343/evesham/); [translated estate entries, based on Williams and Martin's edition](https://www.badseysociety.uk/directories-and-other-records/1086-domesday-book).

Abbot **Walter** remains relevant: the Worcester chronicle places his death on **20 January 1104**. The Trust's detailed building account also uses a late-eleventh-to-1104 chronology. Its abbreviated abbot table instead puts his death in 1086 and gives a different subsequent sequence. Prefer the chronicle for this date; do not build a 1101 cast from that inconsistent modern table. [Worcester chronicle, 1104 annal](https://www.melocki.org.uk/worcester/Chronicle.html); [Trust's detailed account](https://www.eveshamabbey.org.uk/History/Building_the_Abbey.html); [conflicting table](https://www.eveshamabbey.org.uk/History/Abbots_of_Evesham.html).

**Quest geography — story inference.** Use an abbey precinct, a parish, river meadows, a documented kind of mill economy, and outlying agricultural holdings. A care household can be modest and openly fictional. Greed could appear through withheld grain, dishonest measurement, extortion, or refusal to meet an obligation. Goodness can appear through truthful accounts, shared labor, patient care, and protection of a dependant.

**Agency and tradeoff.** Guarin should belong to a nearby knightly manor, with its relationship to abbey or secular interests explicitly invented and bounded. Making him master of the monastery's town would distort the premise. The abbey can be both a powerful landlord and a place where particular people serve Christ well. Avoid importing its famous later institutional scandals or the **1265** battle into the working season. Its strength is a recognizable community and meaningful practical action, not an automatically wicked abbey.

## 7. Ely: an island community with old devotion

**Why it serves the arc.** Boats, crossings, guidance, and provisions make dependence on neighbors tangible. Receiving help while protecting others can strengthen Guarin's conviction and fellowship.

**Historical support.** The religious community honors Saint Etheldreda, with a **673** foundation tradition and Benedictine refoundation in **970**. Work on the present great church began in the **1080s**; the cathedral's own page gives both 1081 and 1083. More decisively for the plot, Ely becomes a bishopric only in **1109**. In 1101 it is an abbey town, not a cathedral city under a Bishop of Ely. This investigation did not independently settle the precise incumbent abbot and local administration for the campaign season. [Ely Cathedral, history and timeline](https://www.elycathedral.org/about/history-heritage/the-story-of-ely).

The Chronicle's **1071** annal describes the king surrounding the island stronghold, a constructed crossing, surrender, and Hereward's escape with followers. This supplies specific memory of conquest and resistance. The later heroic elaborations must not become a complete historical biography, and an old siege does not establish continuing devastation thirty years later. [*Anglo-Saxon Chronicle*, 1071 annal](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/657/pg657-images.html).

**Quest geography — story inference.** Build around inhabited higher ground, waterways, fisheries, agricultural holdings, and constrained routes. Exact 1101 channels, wetland limits, and crossing positions need reconstruction; the modern drained landscape is not the medieval map. A local mill or grain shortage requires its own evidence or an explicit fictional placement. Water access can create problems of care, transport, and predation without making illness a mysterious contagion spread by every traveler.

**Agency and tradeoff.** Guarin's home should be an invented household in the wider monastic district. A corrupt estate agent or armed band can threaten neighbors, while the religious community and villagers supply allies. The water geography is distinctive but changes combat travel and the castle-household setup more than Lewes or Castle Acre. Choose it when dependence, cooperation, and an island parish's bonds are central; do not choose it merely for a famous modern cathedral.

## 8. Durham: powerful institutions, harder personal scale

**Why it serves the arc.** Cuthbert's community gives faith deep local roots. Pilgrims, monks, workers, and episcopal servants can show both Christian service and misuse of power. Particular acts of care make goodness visible within formidable institutions.

**Historical support.** Cuthbert's community arrived in **995**. The castle was ordered in **1072**, after violent northern conflict; current university interpretation presents that setting alongside evidence of an earlier inhabited town. Do not transfer its whole later account of palatine powers to 1101. [Durham University, early town and castle history](https://stories.durham.ac.uk/durham-castle-history/).

**Ranulf Flambard** is bishop from **1099–1128**. He escaped imprisonment in **1101**, joined the invading side, and later made peace with Henry. That is genuine political instability; the exact state of his local agents through the chosen weeks needs care. Prior **Turgot** is securely named in the 1093 construction chronology. The new cathedral is underway, and Cuthbert's translation into it occurs only in **1104**. [Cathedral, Flambard's career](https://www.durhamcathedral.co.uk/explore/the-cathedral-building-and-grounds/the-chapter-house/medieval-graves); [Roger Stalley, documentary chronology](https://projects.mcah.columbia.edu/medieval-architecture/htm/sr/ma_sr_discuss_dc_build.htm).

**Quest geography — story inference.** Use a nearby fictional parish as home, with journeys to the peninsula, religious precinct, agricultural holdings, and wooded or upland routes. The bishopric's officers can make a local conflict feel formidable. Do not automatically populate the map with its later network of castles, hunting establishments, or stone bridges.

**Agency and tradeoff.** Protecting neighbors and gaining religious allies fits Guarin's role; solving the bishopric's politics is a larger undertaking. The Saint Giles hospital dates to **1112**, and the cathedral dates the first stone bridge to **1120**. Neither can anchor a 1101 quest. [Historic England, list entry 1159991, Details](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1159991); [cathedral chronology](https://www.durhamcathedral.co.uk/explore/the-cathedral-building-and-grounds/the-chapter-house/medieval-graves). Keep the victory centered on Guarin's people.

## Adapting the story without forcing the old mechanisms

The required early prayer can occur at an **invented parish or roadside stone cross** known to Guarin before his departure. None of these candidates requires a surviving modern market cross to stand in for it. Rain is scene weather, not evidence of a documented 1101 catastrophe. The emotional center is his sincere plea and its real answer through Christ; the cross's construction material does not choose the town.

The hometown should contain an identifiable circle of people whose lives the player can affect: his sister, an old household servant, a priest, a miller's or farmer's family, and neighbors with mixed loyalties. Their particular deeds are fiction. Give them choices that reveal love and courage alongside greed and fear. A sick person can be grateful, brave, selfish, or frightened; sickness alone never supplies wickedness or a combat target.

For the three finalists, a useful **provisional campaign circuit** is:

| Story function | Lewes | Castle Acre | Shrewsbury |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home and first people saved | Fictional nearby manor and parish | Fictional subordinate manor or local household | Fictional manor outside the main town |
| Wider human stakes | Town households, servants, tradespeople, travelers | Agricultural neighbors and a small settlement | Townspeople and families facing coercive demands |
| Christian help | Parish and Cluniac neighbors; modest care household if desired | Parish and small Cluniac community | Parish and Benedictine abbey community |
| Human evil to confront | Exploitative officer, dishonest supplier, frightened collaborator | Abusive estate agent, predatory household, corrupt local patron | Armed officials or an estate holder protected by stronger power |
| Geography joining the struggle | River corridor, town approaches, hills, rural holdings | Short valley circuit of parish, farms, crossing, and religious house | River-loop approaches, crossing, religious neighborhood, rural routes |
| Visible fulfillment | People helped cooperate and resume ordinary life | Named neighbors endure and help rebuild | Fearful people find courage; protected families have a future |

These are design proposals, not documented local incidents. Keep the final supernatural foothold and victory local enough that the survival of these people is a real result. A royal settlement or a later revolt need not be defeated by the player for that victory to matter.

**Care does not require an Antonine transplant.** This investigation established no English Antonine foundation for 1101. Benedictine or Cluniac neighbors, a parish, a noble household, and an openly fictional charitable community can carry the care story. Anthony can remain a devotional reference if wanted, but a mature named order, its developed treatments, uniform, and wide hospital network need separate dating. The [orders report](../1101-royans/christian-orders.md) sets out the original foundation traditions and later developments.

Likewise, retain ergot only if it helps the chosen English story. A contaminated store or supplier can be local fiction; no opened source establishes an outbreak at these towns in autumn 1101. Regional crop evidence does not justify treating every English field as rye. The [provisions report](technology-and-provisions.md) distinguishes broad archaeological crop patterns, watermills, and dated estate resources. The evil could instead exploit hunger, extortion, failed duties, or a false claim of protection. The core remains Guarin's strengthened will, hope, protection of people, and meaningful defeat of evil.

## Separate alternative: circa 1150

**This is a possible different campaign, not a revised date.** A return after the **Second Crusade, 1147–1149**, can combine the experience of a failed expedition with domestic conflicts under Stephen and Matilda. The royal historical account describes local feuds and changing allegiance but explicitly says anarchy did not cover the whole country. This date is useful if that combination better expresses Guarin's initial despair; it is not preferable because buildings are older or more complete. [OpenStax, crusading chronology](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/13-4-the-crusading-movement); [Royal Household, Stephen and Matilda](https://www.royal.uk/stephen-and-matilda).

| Candidate | Narrative gain around 1150 | New responsibility for the adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Lewes | The Warenne family's experience of the same later crusade can make loss and an uncertain household future close to home | Replace the 1101 succession context and research the post-1148 household; do not retain William II as living lord |
| Castle Acre | William III's death on the Second Crusade in **1148** gives a specific patron-family loss around which ordinary dependants must continue living | The inheritance and agents change; a homecoming after this crusade needs new companions and war memories |
| Shrewsbury | The **1138** royal siege and contested custody give recent domestic suffering a concrete local history | Research the 1150 sheriff, castle custody, and local alliances; Robert of Bellême's 1101 English power is long past |

The Warenne death is documented in the [keeper's family history](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/castle-acre-castle-and-bailey-gate/history/); the 1138 siege is discussed in [Baker's archaeological report, p. 4](https://castlestudiestrust.org/docs/SHREWSBURY-CASTLE-19-REPORT-1-20.pdf). These facts explain changed human relationships and political pressure. They do not establish every nearby village's suffering or require a story centered on the succession.

The Chronicle's retrospective civil-war passage reports people saying **“Christ slept, and his saints”**, then praises Abbot Martin's charity and provision for guests amid the suffering. That juxtaposition is a useful literary parallel for despair answered through visible Christian goodness. It is a chronicler's account spanning nineteen winters, not a neutral local census or proof of divine absence. It belongs to the later alternative, never to 1101 dialogue as a report of events already experienced. [*Anglo-Saxon Chronicle*, 1137 entry and ensuing retrospective account](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/657/pg657-images.html).

## Brief reconstruction notes

After selecting the narrative setting, distinguish an early institution from its complete surviving fabric. At Castle Acre, the priory probably moved around **1100**, church consecration followed in **1146–1148**, and major town-defense replanning is attributed to the period after **1138**. These dates guide reconstruction. [Priory chronology](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/castle-acre-castle-acre-priory/history/), [castle chronology](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/castle-acre-castle-and-bailey-gate/history/).

Rochester's great keep begins after **1127**, while the hospital chapel's completion is around **1120**. Richmond's great keep is also later twelfth-century work. Lewes's early shell-keep dating is disputed and its polygonal towers are thirteenth-century. Shrewsbury's standing principal castle building is thirteenth-century. The cited profiles and archaeological reports distinguish these phases; neither the present ruins nor their building materials determine the story recommendation. ## Recommendation, source checks, and remaining limits

**Prefer Lewes** for a campaign that grows from a known parish into a wider community Guarin can save. It supplies the broadest combination of relationships, local authority, Christian neighbors, and connected rural and town problems. Invent the small family manor, identify the people he loves, and make their survival and cooperation the measure of victory.

**Prefer Castle Acre instead** if the intended emotional effect depends on a smaller circle of repeatedly encountered people. **Prefer Shrewsbury instead** if confronting predatory armed power should be more prominent. Rochester remains the strongest fourth option when a historical early care foundation is particularly useful. None requires a date change for the governing Christian arc.

The most decision-relevant sources are the town survey and monastic foundations for the leading candidates, archaeological and chronicle evidence for Shrewsbury, and Rochester's hospital record. The prayer, its real divine answer, and meaningful victory are governing narrative requirements; research supplies people, institutions, pressures, and geography around them.

Material limits to carry forward:

- **Lewes hospital:** the Trust's circa-1085 date is reported tradition. Christopher Whittick, *The History of the hospital of St Nicholas, Lewes and its successors*, *Sussex Archaeological Collections* 148 (2010), 111–127, was identified but its full text was not retrieved. It is named here without an unread link.
- **Warenne ownership:** the early biography describes forfeiture in the 1101 crisis, but its exact late-1101 transition remains unsettled in the checked evidence. Do not build a plot on uninterrupted possession or an invented exact confiscation day.
- **Richmond and Ely:** precise local 1101 leadership and administrative detail remain less securely established than the existence of their institutions. Verify them before assigning historical people decisive fictional actions.
- **Care and production:** exact 1101 bed numbers, treatments, household stocks, crop ratios, working mill sites, travel durations, and charitable incidents were not recovered for all candidates. Suggested quest circuits and individual good or evil deeds are story inference.
- **Source access:** the full Lewes council survey exceeded the web reader's size limit; hosted extracts, direct download, and Whittick's full text were blocked. They are not cited as examined evidence. Supplied historical links were opened and their relevant content reviewed.
- **Maps:** all eight town destinations were reviewed. They provide modern orientation; no unverified institution-specific Maps destination is supplied.
- **Claims not established:** an England-wide 1101 ruin, famine, weather catastrophe, occult network, local ergot epidemic, or a commercial sales advantage for the English setting. Those must not be inferred from this comparison.

There is **no blocker to the ranked setting recommendation**. Selecting a town would begin a separate, narrower reconstruction of Guarin's fictional manor, household relationships, local authority, care arrangements, and quest routes. It would not itself authorize relocation of the current story or a switch to circa 1150.
