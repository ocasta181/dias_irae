# Non-Christian religions, religious dissent, and alleged cults in 1101

This report uses late summer to winter **1101** as its date. Its local frame is the [Royans](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Royans%2C+France), the [Isère](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Is%C3%A8re+valley%2C+France), the [Drôme](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Dr%C3%B4me+valley%2C+France), and the [Rhône corridor](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Rh%C3%B4ne+valley%2C+France), in the region later called [Dauphiné](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Dauphin%C3%A9%2C+France). The present-day place names locate the evidence; they do not imply modern political borders in 1101.

**Documented** means supported by a text, object, or specialist study. **Alleged** means an accusation preserved in a source. **Later** means that the familiar form belongs after 1101. **Story inference** means a proposed fictional use. An enemy's description can establish that a rumor circulated without establishing that its accusation was true.

## What was present, and how near?

| Tradition or claim | Position in 1101 | Relevance to the story |
|---|---|---|
| Judaism | Living communities; regional history well documented | Strongest non-Christian regional anchor; exact Royans households unverified |
| Western Islam | Living states and communities | Elsewhere in [Europe](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Europe) and through [Mediterranean](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Mediterranean+Sea) travel and crusader memory |
| Nizari Ismailism | Recently established movement | Distant; no local European network established |
| Slavic and Baltic religions | Still living in particular northern and eastern regions | Remote contemporary comparison, not evidence of local survival |
| Norse religion | Conversion advanced; some practices and memories survived | Mixed transition, not an intact common northern cult |
| Local charms and ritual magic | Present within Christian society | Locally plausible; not automatically a separate religion |
| Mithraism and other Roman cults | Ancient religion; living medieval continuation unsupported | Material ruins, reused stones, and invented interpretations |
| Bogomils | Living Christian dissent in the southeast | Possible distant reports; local transmission unproved |
| Cathars and Waldensians | Familiar organized forms belong later | Chronological exclusions for 1101 |
| Satanic covens, Templar Baphomet, modern Wicca | Familiar forms belong much later | Later legend or explicit fictional invention |

The following entries supply the evidence behind these distinctions.

## Judaism: established communities, local limits, and persecution

**Existence and history.** [Vienne](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Vienne%2C+Is%C3%A8re%2C+France) has a stronger historical foundation than an invented isolated village congregation. Bernhard Blumenkranz records collective Jewish ownership of a town plot in 849, a named Jewish quarter in the late tenth century, and Jewish estates and vineyards near the city. Property dealings sometimes involved the Church. A Jew named Astier served as an abbot's steward near the end of the tenth century. These establish regional settlement and ordinary economic relationships; they do not identify Astier as alive in 1101. [Encyclopaedia Judaica entry](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/vienne).

[Lyon](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Lyon%2C+France) also had a documented ninth-century community. Bishop Agobard's hostile campaigns and royal protection show both pressure and negotiated security. The older reference article is useful for this history, but it cannot supply a census of the community in 1101. Its later badge rules must not be moved into this earlier year. [Historical reference entry](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10212-lyons).

**Leadership and culture.** Rabbinic study, household observance, communal institutions, and relations with local rulers matter more than a single European leader. Rashi (Rabbi Solomon son of Isaac), who lived from 1040 to 1105, was active at [Troyes](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Troyes%2C+France) during the story. His school offers a real contemporary point of reference for biblical and Talmudic study; the Talmud is the body of rabbinic discussion of law and interpretation. He was not the head of a Royans congregation. [Community history](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/14528-troyes); [biographical entry](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/12585-rashi-solomon-bar-isaac).

**Persecution.** The massacres of 1096 in the [Rhineland](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Rhineland%2C+Germany) were recent events in 1101. Christian chroniclers describe Emicho's followers attacking Jews at [Mainz](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Mainz%2C+Germany), killings, plunder, and coercive conversion despite attempted episcopal protection. These are partisan narratives, not reliable audited casualty counts. They nevertheless document serious violence; Albert also condemns greed and forced conversion. Do not assign these acts to every crusader. [Albert and Ekkehard, translated primary accounts](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/1096jews.asp).

**Symbols and myth.** The six-pointed star existed in several religious and decorative settings. Its later status as a general Jewish emblem should not determine every 1101 costume or doorway. A major communal flag example dates to [Prague](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Prague%2C+Czech+Republic) in 1354. Medieval amulets also complicate any simple division between “religious” and “magical” signs. [Specialist history of the Magen David](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/magen-david).

**Story inference.** A traveler with business or kinship links to Vienne is more defensible than a named local community without a record. Fear after 1096 could affect trust, travel, and what a survivor will tell Guarin. Give that character a household and motives. Accusations of secret demonic rites would be persecution or fictional manipulation, not a fact about Judaism.

## Islam: several societies, not the crusader's imagined pantheon

Islam was a living monotheistic religion. Sunni and Shia traditions arose from disputes over succession after Muhammad and developed further differences in religious authority. Both revered the Quran. “Allah” means God in Arabic. Islamic art also varied: religious settings often avoided figures, while courtly objects could show people and animals. [Museum introduction](https://www.metmuseum.org/learn/educators/curriculum-resources/art-of-the-islamic-world/introduction/frequently-asked-questions).

### Sunni Islam under the Almoravids

**Existence and leadership.** On the [Iberian Peninsula](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Iberian+Peninsula), Yusuf ibn Tashfin's Almoravid power was current in 1101. The dynasty developed in the eleventh century and took control of Muslim principalities in [al-Andalus](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Al-Andalus%2C+Iberian+Peninsula) from 1090. Its rulers promoted the Maliki school, a Sunni tradition of law. Their early criticism of princely luxury did not prevent later Almoravid courts from adopting rich artistic forms. The Almohads belong to a later twelfth-century phase. [Museum historical survey](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-art-of-the-almoravid-and-almohad-periods-ca-1062-1269).

**Symbols, influence, and story inference.** Use a particular ruler, inscription, textile, or legal tradition rather than a universal “Muslim faction” emblem. A western Muslim trader or a report of Iberian politics would have a different background from an Egyptian official or an eastern soldier. Travel into the Royans remains an inference requiring a credible route, language, patron, and purpose; these sources do not document such a visitor in 1101.

### Muslims under Norman rule in [Sicily](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sicily%2C+Italy)

**Existence and history.** The Norman conquest of Sicily ended in 1091. This did not erase its Muslim inhabitants. Jeremy Johns describes a conquest in which negotiation and alliance with Muslim communities often mattered as much as battles. Muslim leaders had their own disputes and could seek Norman intervention. In 1101 the island therefore offers Muslim lives under Christian rulers, not simply a surviving independent Muslim kingdom. [Specialist study of the conquest](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/arabic-administration-in-norman-sicily/when-first-the-normans-crossed-into-sicily/6D9AE93108C86EDB9A44F49D693BDB4F).

**Leadership and culture.** Distinguish Christian sovereignty from local Muslim religious or communal authority. No single caliph headed all island residents. Earlier dynastic allegiance does not establish the beliefs of every household in 1101.

**Story inference.** A memory of interpreters, negotiated surrender, or neighboring Christian and Muslim households can complicate Guarin's categories. An imported object may carry Arabic writing without being a cult implement. A returning soldier could mistake an ordinary inscription for a spell; the narrator need not endorse him.

### Fatimid Ismaili Shia Islam

**Existence, history, and symbols.** The Fatimid dynasty, 909–1171, claimed descent from Muhammad through Fatima and held an Ismaili Shia conception of authority. Its caliphate competed with the Sunni Abbasids. [Cairo](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Cairo%2C+Egypt), founded in 969, was its capital. Mediterranean trade carried luxury goods; inscribed *tiraz* textiles could name a ruler. Animal or human imagery on courtly objects did not make their makers polytheists. [Museum survey](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-art-of-the-fatimid-period-909-1171).

**Leadership in 1101.** Al-Musta‘li was caliph as the year began; the vizier al-Afdal held great practical power. Al-Amir succeeded as a child in 1101. This basic sequence is also given in an older dynastic history, whose broad judgments should be treated cautiously. [Historical chronology](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/63391/pg63391-images.html).

**Story inference.** A report from the eastern Mediterranean may concern this state rather than the Almoravids. Do not merge Fatimid officials, Sunni enemies, and Nizari agents into one coordinated force. Their conflicts offer better historical texture than a common secret agenda.

## Nizari Ismailis: a real movement and a later “Assassin” legend

**Existence and leadership.** Hasan-i Sabbah took [Alamut](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Alamut%2C+Iran) in 1090 in present-day [Iran](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Iran). He was already an Ismaili missionary; the specifically Nizari division followed the Fatimid succession dispute of 1094. He supported Nizar against al-Musta‘li. Hasan led the missionary organization and represented an imam, a religious leader believed to possess rightful spiritual authority; he did not claim to be that imam himself. Fortresses, teaching, and political survival mattered alongside selected assassinations. He remained alive until 1124. [Farhad Daftary's specialist biography](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/hasan-sabbah/).

**Area and chronology.** This movement existed in 1101, but it was not an established local European cult. Expansion into [Syria](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Syria) began in the early twelfth century, and secure Syrian strongholds developed later. Sources here provide no basis for an Alamut-controlled network in the Royans.

**Fact and legend.** Targeted killings were real. The familiar stories of a leader drugging recruits, taking them into an artificial paradise, and sending them out obediently belong to later hostile and European storytelling. The celebrated Syrian leader Rashid al-Din Sinan was active in 1163–1193. Daftary traces the development of these legends through later medieval accounts, including Marco Polo, rather than treating hashish accusations as proof of systematic drug conditioning. [Specialist analysis of the Assassin legend](https://www.iis.ac.uk/scholarly-contributions/assassin/).

**Symbols and story inference.** A white-robed, uniform dagger order with a timeless emblem is not established here. If Guarin hears of distant assassinations in 1101, make the information incomplete and its route explicit. A detailed “Old Man of the Mountain” paradise tale would be a later legend transplanted into the story. Naming this distinction allows deliberate invention without calling it contemporary evidence.

## Pagan survivals: different regions and different rates of change

### Slavic religions beyond Christian frontiers

**Existence and history.** Some Slavic populations east of the [Elbe](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Elbe%2C+Germany) retained non-Christian religious institutions around 1101. Rebellions in 983 and 1066 show how conversion, political authority, and local divisions interacted. A Christian raid in 1068 targeted the sanctuary associated with [Rethra](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Rethra+region%2C+Mecklenburg%2C+Germany) and its sacred horse; the exact sanctuary location is uncertain, so the map is regional. A crusading appeal in 1108 and the Wendish Crusade of 1147 are later than the story. [John Eldevik's historical study](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/8AD76523CF2B5E82DE65E190F6A4FAA9/S036215291600012Xa.pdf/saints-pagans-and-the-wonders-of-the-east-the-medieval-imaginary-and-its-manuscript-contexts.pdf).

**Leadership, symbols, and allegations.** Sanctuary priests, rulers, and competing local groups matter; “the Slavs” were not one pagan church. Sacred horses and named shrines appear through Christian accounts. Adam of Bremen names the deity Redigost in his account of the 1066 revolt, alleging that a slain bishop's head was offered to him. This identifies a reported god and sacrifice; the hostile narrative requires scrutiny. [Eldevik's analysis of Adam's account](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/8AD76523CF2B5E82DE65E190F6A4FAA9/S036215291600012Xa.pdf/saints-pagans-and-the-wonders-of-the-east-the-medieval-imaginary-and-its-manuscript-contexts.pdf).

**Story inference.** Such stories could reach a traveler through imperial contacts. They cannot establish a local forest temple. A frightened listener may apply a distant report to an unfamiliar local custom; that mistake can itself drive a scene.

### Baltic religions

**Existence and area.** Non-Christian religions remained important around the [Baltic Sea](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Baltic+Sea), including [Lithuania](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Lithuania), long after 1101. Mindaugas's baptism in 1251 and the major conversion of Lithuania in 1387 illustrate the much later chronology. Military orders and their thirteenth-century campaigns must not be assumed present already in the story year. [Darius Baronas's study, English abstract](https://www.lituanistika.lt/content/6187).

**Beliefs and rites, with later evidence limits.** Perkūnas is the Baltic thunder, lightning, and storm god. [Specialist reference entry](https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/perkunas/). A myth recorded in 1261 links cremation with worship of Andojas, Perkūnas, Kalvelis, and Žvėrūna. Archaeologist Vykintas Vaitkevičius relates it to twelfth- and thirteenth-century burial changes and the emerging state's religious order. This supplies concrete gods and a mortuary rite, but a text written 160 years after the story cannot establish that exact list, ritual, or state hierarchy in 1101. [Study's English abstract](https://www.lituanistika.lt/content/15898).

**Leadership and symbols.** This establishes survival, not a single Baltic hierarchy, uniform pantheon, or emblem. The sources consulted do not identify a particular Lithuanian religious leader or standard in 1101. Later written god-lists cannot simply become a complete contemporary handbook.

**Story inference.** A remote northern origin would need explanation before such a character entered the campaign region. Do not use “pagan north” as a loose origin for any mysterious symbol. Uncertainty about the specific community is a research limit, not permission to combine unrelated gods.

### Norse belief after conversion

**Existence and change.** In [Denmark](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Denmark), Harald's conversion claim appears on the royal stone of about 965, and most people were Christian by around 1050. Conversion nevertheless took time, and practices could change meaning rather than disappear at once. A smith's mold capable of making both crosses and Thor's hammers provides material evidence of overlapping demand. Other northern regions followed different timelines. [National museum overview](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/religion-magic-death-and-rituals/christianity-comes-to-denmark/).

**Beliefs and rites.** The earlier religion honored many powers. Odin was associated with war and the dead, Thor with thunder and his hammer, and Frey with harvest and fertility; Freyja was associated with love and fertility. Sacrificial ceremonies sought the gods' favor. These are a museum's synthesis of objects and literary traditions, not a record of every Danish household in 1101. Individual saga details require their own text dates before use as contemporary testimony. [National museum deity survey](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/religion-magic-death-and-rituals/the-viking-gods/).

**Leadership and symbols.** Thor's hammer is a defensible period object when tied to archaeological context. It does not prove that its owner belonged to an organized anti-Christian society. No common “Viking occult order” follows from the evidence.

**Story inference.** An old pendant, inherited story, or uneasy Christian convert is more useful than an intact northern hierarchy operating locally. Guarin could misread a keepsake; an informed character could explain why an object and its owner's present faith need not agree.

## Local folk practice, magic, and astrology

**Existence and evidence.** Christian Europe contained charms, incantations, knots, and ritual uses of ordinary materials. Church correction texts, including Burchard's early eleventh-century work, condemned practices their authors regarded as improper. Sophie Page emphasizes that magic was not confined to outsiders: religious people also used or debated it. Condemnation records what a cleric sought to prohibit, not necessarily the exact frequency or meaning of every act. [Scholarly chapter on medieval magic](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10161191/1/Page_OxfordconceptsAugust2020.pdf).

**Leadership and symbols.** A healer or knowledgeable neighbor need not lead a separate pagan congregation. A charm could invoke Christian language while being condemned by another Christian. An old spring, tree, or stone is not itself evidence for an unbroken druid organization. The consulted sources do not establish a named local ritual specialist in 1101.

**Astrology.** Learned interpretation of the heavens and harmful sorcery were not identical categories. Translating Greek and Arabic learning enlarged the Latin repertoire from the late eleventh century, with major developments during the twelfth and thirteenth. Do not equip every 1101 village practitioner with the later full range of learned manuals. [Page's discussion of learned traditions](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10161191/1/Page_OxfordconceptsAugust2020.pdf).

**Story inference.** Someone can tie a knot against sickness, consult a calendrical sign, or repeat a Christian charm without belonging to a non-Christian religion. Show the act, its claimed purpose, and the accuser separately. For the story's rye illness, competing explanations can coexist: bad grain, punishment, a neighbor's malice, or an actual supernatural cause supplied by the fiction. None establishes an organized historical coven.

## Mithraism and Roman remains: strong material possibility, no medieval continuity

**Existence and history.** Roman Mithraism belongs to antiquity. A temple in [Roman London](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=London%2C+United+Kingdom) was used approximately 240–350, with the familiar image of Mithras killing a bull, called the *tauroctony*. The surviving archaeology does not supply a complete insider account of the mysteries. [Museum temple history](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/roman-londons-temple-mithras/). David Walsh's research places its varied decline in late antiquity, studying roughly 270–430; this is not evidence of a living 1101 institution. [Archaeological thesis](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/56837/).

**Leadership, culture, and symbols.** Initiation, communal meals, and small groups of men are better models than a medieval knightly order. Inscribed vessels from [Angers](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Angers%2C+France) include a dedication by Pylades, enslaved within the imperial administrative system: adherents were not exclusively soldiers. Greek and Latin inscriptions, banquet pottery, and the formula *Deo Invicto Mithrae* belong to ancient evidence. They are not the motto of a verified medieval brotherhood. [Museum's inscription study](https://ow-mba.angers.fr/fr/expo-grave-inscriptions-religieuses).

**Regional anchor.** A real rock-cut Mithraic relief survives at [Bourg-Saint-Andéol](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Bourg-Saint-And%C3%A9ol%2C+France), farther down the Rhône corridor. The official heritage record identifies the bull-slaying scene. This gives the story a regional material analogy, not proof that the same sanctuary type lay beneath every granary. [Official heritage record](https://www.ardeche-guide.com/patrimoine/bas-relief-antique-du-dieu-mithra-196172/); [historical research on Roman cult evidence](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1981_num_2_1_6798).

**Canon limit and story inference.** The mithraeum below the granary at [La-Motte-Saint-Didier](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=La-Motte-Saint-Didier%2C+Saint-Antoine-l%27Abbaye%2C+France) is story canon. No archaeological attestation for that precise site was located in this research. An abandoned room, reused carved stone, lost inscription, or later Christian fear of an ancient image is plausible as fiction. A continuous Mithraic order with medieval leaders is a different and unsupported claim. Roman and Gallo-Roman gods should likewise appear through material remains or recollection unless a separate survival is demonstrated. The previous supernatural “sealing” in canon is invented history, not a recovered ancient rite.

## Christian dissent: distinguish these groups from non-Christian religions

### Bogomils

**Existence, origins, and geography.** Bogomils were current in 1101 in parts of [Bulgaria](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Bulgaria) and adjacent imperial territories. A tenth-century account by the priest Kosmas describes followers of a priest called Bogomil. Surviving sources are sparse and often hostile. Dualism, the explanation of the world through opposed spiritual powers, matters in their reported beliefs; it does not make them worshippers of the evil power. They understood themselves within Christianity. [Scholarly survey](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-christian-heresy/bogomils/7CE28C6F475CCF7D0E3F7CDF29DDFADB).

**Leadership and allegations.** Anna Komnene describes Basil and twelve associates at [Constantinople](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Constantinople%2C+Istanbul%2C+Turkey), an imperial trap to obtain his teachings, and his execution by burning. Her account presents the emperor as defender of the faith and uses demonic language against opponents. The episode's chronology should not be pinned confidently to 1101 here. [Alexiad, book XV, translated primary text](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/basis/AnnaComnena-Alexiad15.asp).

**Story inference.** A crusader might remember a denunciation of eastern heretics. No source consulted establishes a Bogomil congregation in the Royans. Use the remembered accusation as that speaker's belief; do not turn Anna's polemic into evidence for a local Satanic network or invent a universal Bogomil badge.

### Cathars: later evidence and a modern historical debate

**Chronology.** The familiar western communities labeled Cathar become clearly visible after 1101, with important evidence in the 1140s and later. Their later ascetic ranks and rites must not become an established regional organization in the story year. Older syntheses describe dualist teachings and developed communities but sometimes classify them as “non-Christian” by doctrinal judgment. That wording should not obscure their Christian texts and claims. [Reference overview](https://www.encyclopedia.com/philosophy-and-religion/christianity/christianity-general/cathars).

**Fact and reconstruction.** Historians debate how coherent a common Cathar church or identity was and how far persecutors constructed the category. This does not erase the persecution or mean that no religious dissent existed. [Scholarly review of the debate](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/22951). A hostile account assigning corrupt sexual behavior to all adherents is evidence of denunciation, not independent verification. [Translated polemical account](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/heresy1.asp).

**Story inference.** A particular person can challenge clerical conduct without being a “Cathar bishop.” Any supposed 1101 organization needs evidence of its own, not a later name applied backward. No contemporary local leadership, motto, or standard was established in this research.

### Waldensians: a later Christian poverty movement

**Chronology and leadership.** Waldo's preaching movement emerged at Lyon around 1170, roughly seventy years after the story. A later chronicle places his conversion in 1173; this difference argues for an approximate date, not artificial precision. Poverty, Gospel teaching, and lay preaching are central. These were Christian claims, not remnants of a prehistoric Alpine cult. [Museum history](https://museeprotestant.org/en/notice/a-history-of-the-waldensians/); [translated conversion account](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/waldo1.asp).

**Story inference.** Poor laypeople, criticism of wealth, and charitable action are available themes in 1101. Calling such characters Waldensians, giving them a later emblem, or linking them to established mountain communities would require changing the chronology. This is a later movement, not an available faction in the present setting.

## Satanic covens, Baphomet, and other later constructions

**Witch covens.** Fear of harmful magic does not establish a coordinated religion of devil worship. The developed witch-sabbath image took shape much later; research discussed by Hanna Walsdorf places the emergence of the dancing sabbath fiction around 1400. Nothing consulted establishes organized Satanic covens in the 1101 Royans. This is a bounded evidence statement, not proof about every undocumented person's private act. [Academic discussion](https://www.new.ox.ac.uk/church-dance-and-witches-sabbath).

**Templars and Baphomet.** The Templars themselves formed about 1119–1120; William of Tyre's older account gives 1118. The [orders report](christian-orders.md) sets out that chronology. Accusations of idol worship belong to their proceedings of 1307–1312, with coercion and torture materially affecting the evidence. Their suppression does not verify a secret ancient idol religion. [Early foundation narrative](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/tyre-templars.asp); [Helen Nicholson's history of the proceedings](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/knights-templar/6FB2D8E32CF97F1F5D6F303EB646450B/listing). The familiar winged goat-headed Baphomet is Éliphas Lévi's nineteenth-century construction, published in the 1854–1856 period. Its symbolism is not an attested eleventh-century cult image. [Julian Strube's study](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/10900/141891/1/Strube_019.pdf).

**Crusader literature.** The *Song of Roland* assigns “Saracens” invented gods including Apollin, Mahumet, and Tervagant. That is Christian literary polemic, not Muslim theology. The poem alone does not establish either that every crusader knew it or that Apollin was the biblical Abaddon/Apollyon; that equation requires an interpretive argument. [Primary text in translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/391/pg391.html).

**Modern exclusions.** Modern Wicca belongs to the twentieth-century work associated with Gerald Gardner. Modern druid revival developed much later than the medieval setting. Neither supplies a continuous, documented 1101 local organization. [Scholarly study of Gardner](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/abs/gerald-gardner-and-the-creation-of-wicca/3A84FE399D63CEA37C363E1DEB380E75); [revival history, a modern order's own account](https://druidry.org/resources/the-druid-renaissance).

**Story inference.** A fictional demon can recruit people in 1101. Give that network its own invented history and signs. Attaching it to an actual Jewish, Muslim, or dissident Christian population converts polemic into supposed fact and weakens the historical distinction the story can exploit.

## What is current, ascendant, waning, and later

**Current:** regional Jewish life; varied Muslim societies elsewhere; Christian charms and arguments about magic; Bogomil dissent farther east; some northern pagan institutions. **Ascendant:** Almoravid power and the recently established Nizari movement. **Waning or transformed:** Norse religious institutions in converted areas; ancient cult sites long reduced to remains. **Later:** Waldensian organization, familiar Cathar institutions, the mature Assassin legend, witch-sabbath conspiracies, Templar idol trials, Lévi's Baphomet, and modern pagan revivals. These labels describe the particular regions and evidence above, not a uniform European sequence.

The principal canon checks are the unverified La-Motte mithraeum, the interpretation of Apollin as Abaddon, and any organized medieval pagan or Satanic network. Before assigning a named local Jewish household or sanctuary, consult regional charters and archaeological inventories. Before writing a detailed eastern memory, fix its date, place, speaker, and source of information. A primary accusation, an ancient object, and a later legend are three different kinds of evidence; keeping them distinct gives the fiction more credible uncertainty.
