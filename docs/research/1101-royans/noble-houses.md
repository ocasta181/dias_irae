# Noble houses in the story’s region, 1101

Research scope: late summer through winter 1101, from [Royans](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Royans%2C+France) and the [Isère valley](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Is%C3%A8re+valley%2C+France) into the [Drôme valley](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Dr%C3%B4me+valley%2C+France) and [Rhône corridor](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Rh%C3%B4ne+valley%2C+France). These modern map links locate the setting; they do not show medieval borders. Research checked on 5 October 2026.

## Political and visual rules for 1101

This is territory in modern [France](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=France), but most of the setting belonged to the [kingdom of Burgundy-Arles](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kingdom+of+Arles) within the Holy Roman Empire. The imperial succession followed King Rudolf III’s death in 1032. Local power depended on castles, estates, church offices and negotiated rights. The future [Dauphiné](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Dauphin%C3%A9%2C+France) was still being assembled. It was not yet a single province governed through a settled hierarchy. [Isère departmental archives, *Chroniques d’Archives* 9, pp. 10–12](https://culture.isere.fr/sites/isere-culture-fr/files/2020-01/Chroniques-d-Archives-numero-9.pdf#page=10).

Hereditary coats of arms developed in the twelfth century. An old family can therefore exist in 1101 without possessing its familiar later shield. A seal can prove a design at a particular date, but its wax color does not establish the shield’s colors. Neither arms nor a family motto proves a uniform worn by every servant. [Bibliothèque nationale de France, *L’Armorial*, p. 4](https://classes.bnf.fr/pdf/MF_fiche_2.pdf#page=4).

The heraldic terms used below are **or**: gold or yellow; **argent**: silver or white; **azure**: blue; **gules**: red; **field**: the shield’s background; **bend**: a diagonal stripe; **chief**: a horizontal band across the top; **saltire**: an X-shaped arrangement. An **allod** is property held in ownership without an obligation to a superior lord for that property. A **cartulary** is a collection of copied charters. The Institute for Research and History of Texts (IRHT) publishes the charter database Telma and seal database Sigilla. Each “no verified 1101” finding below means that this research did not establish the item, rather than that the item could never have existed.

The evidence and limits behind each row follow. “Later” does not mean that no ancestor could have lived in 1101. It means that the later institution, combined family name or ruling position must not be placed in that year without more evidence.

## 1. Guigonides: the counts of Albon

**Existence, leadership and title.** The ruler in 1101 is conventionally numbered **Guigues III**, son of Guigues II “the Fat.” Some genealogical schemes include earlier namesakes and change the numerals. Use “Guigues III, count of Albon” consistently, and identify the generation when comparing sources. The *Regeste Dauphinois*, volume I, column 489, act 2865, records a donation by Guigues, count of [Albon](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Albon%2C+Dr%C3%B4me%2C+France), in **1101**. [University Grenoble Alpes scan, PDF page 252](https://bibnum-patrimoniale.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/numerisation/384212101_9629_001.pdf#page=252).

The nickname **Dalphinus** first appears in **1110**, attached to Guigues III’s son, the future Guigues IV. It does not make Guigues III a dauphin in 1101. Bernard Bligny also distinguishes the nickname from the later dolphin emblem; one must not explain the name by projecting the emblem backward. [Bligny, “Note sur l’origine et la signification du terme dauphin,” 1985](https://www.persee.fr/doc/shmes_1261-9078_1985_act_15_1_1444).

**Holdings and culture.** Their growing power linked the Rhône-side family center with the [Grenoble](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Grenoble%2C+France) area and Alpine holdings. The 1101 Royans monastic donation is especially relevant to the route. A case dated “[1101?]” shows a count’s court hearing a dispute over church tithes. This is a household of judges, witnesses and negotiated rights, as well as armed followers. It does not prove that every Royans estate owed the count the same service. [*Regeste*, acts 2865 and 2867](https://bibnum-patrimoniale.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/numerisation/384212101_9629_001.pdf#page=252).

A surviving **31 October 1110** charter gives a close comparison for household practice: Guigues, his wife Mathilde and their sons consent to gifts of land, water, woodland and pasture to [Chalais](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Abbaye+de+Chalais%2C+Voreppe%2C+France). Salvation, family assent and penalties for interference appear together. Mathilde’s royal-sounding designation does not by itself settle her disputed parentage. [IRHT, original Chalais charter, Isère archives 4 H 111](https://telma.irht.cnrs.fr/outils/originaux/charte3757/).

**Colors, arms and motto.** No verified 1101 family palette or motto. Later Dauphiné arms show a blue dolphin on gold, with red details. A **1331** seal of Guigues VIII shows dolphins on his shield and horse equipment: a dated example, not the first use and not Guigues III’s arms. [Sigilla: later blazon](https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/151778), [1331 seal, Côte-d’Or archives B 11919](https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/298).

**Story use:** Albon is an ascendant power. The canon’s general Albon suzerainty over Royans is a narrative simplification; particular lands and rights require particular relationships.

## 2. Princes of Royans and the early Sassenage family

**Royans evidence.** A notice dated **circa 1040**, catalogued without an authenticity warning, names **Ismidon, prince of Royans**. Bishop Artaud confirms an arrangement with [Montmajour abbey](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Abbaye+de+Montmajour%2C+Arles%2C+France). The monks produced documents grounding their rights in an earlier gift to the Roman Church by Ismidon’s father or grandfather. This establishes a regional princely lineage and inherited patronage before 1101. It does not establish the ruler sixty years later, or prove every link in a later Bérenger genealogy. [IRHT, notice from the Grenoble cartulary, A 34](https://telma.irht.cnrs.fr/chartes/116732).

**Sassenage evidence.** The early lords of [Sassenage](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sassenage%2C+France) must be distinguished from Royans’s rulers. **Guillaume de Sassenage** appears among the judges in the Albon court case dated “[1101?].” This is useful evidence of a named family member near the story date, but “judge” does not establish that he was the senior lord. [*Regeste*, act 2867](https://bibnum-patrimoniale.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/numerisation/384212101_9629_001.pdf#page=252).

**The later union.** The archive guide places the Bérenger succession to Sassenage in the **early fourteenth century**: Albert of Sassenage died without a male successor; his nephew Henri, son of Béatrix of Sassenage and Aymard Bérenger of [Pont-en-Royans](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Pont-en-Royans%2C+France), inherited and took the Sassenage name. “Bérenger-Sassenage” is therefore unsuitable as a united 1101 house. [Sassenage archive guide, quoting archivist Yves Soulingeas](https://archives.chateau-de-sassenage.com/guide).

**Culture and influence.** For Royans, monastic gifts, retained episcopal rights and appeals to papal authority are evidenced directly. For Sassenage, service in a greater lord’s court is evidenced near 1101. These support landed households connected by witnesses, kinship and church property. They do not support a fully reconstructed 1101 family tree.

**Colors, arms and motto.** No verified 1101 palette, hereditary shield or motto for either early line. The château’s retrospective history associates later Bérenger-Sassenage arms with a lion borrowed from the princes of Royans. It identifies an armorial drawing in archive item R36 but does not date it to 1101. Its motto **“Si fabula, nobilis illa est”** means “If it is a fable, it is a noble one.” This belongs to later family presentation. [Official château history](https://www.chateau-de-sassenage.com/le-chateau/).

The later published Sassenage palette is **alternating silver and blue horizontal bars, with a red lion and gold crown, tongue and claws**. A modern armorial reproduces an example identified as d’Hozier’s compilation following the **1696** edict, Dauphiné volume, folio 127. This is a retrospective visual reference; the original folio was not independently inspected here. The early Royans palette remains unverified. [Armorial de France, Sassenage entry and cited reproduction](https://armorialdefrance.fr/page_blason.php?ville=13555).

The château cites an alleged **1484** tournament crest depicting **Mélusine**, the supernatural ancestress. Its cited work is actually Chevalier’s **1888** edition of a **1673 fictional narrative**, which explicitly allows invented crests. It is not a contemporary 1484 witness. The later family legend and descent claims must remain separate from the circa 1040 charter. [Château account](https://www.chateau-de-sassenage.com/le-chateau/), [Chevalier, introduction to *Un tournoi à Romans en 1484*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10824/10824-h/10824-h.htm).

**Story use:** Guarin, Almodis and Aimar remain fictional. Ismidon supplies historical background without supplying their pedigree.

## 3. Adhémar: Monteil and the southern corridor

**Existence and leadership.** The Adhémar are central to the later identity of [Montélimar](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Mont%C3%A9limar%2C+France), formerly Monteil. An early regional lineage is plausible, but the exact pedigree and 1101 senior lord are less secure than popular family trees imply. Proposed parentage for the crusading Bishop Adhémar of Monteil varies; even a modern synthesis records alternatives rather than a settled chain. He died in **1098**, so cannot lead a living household in 1101. [Biographical synthesis and references to Chevalier and Bréhier](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adh%C3%A9mar_de_Monteil).

An important source warning comes from a local historical study of [Val des Nymphes](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Val+des+Nymphes%2C+La+Garde-Adh%C3%A9mar%2C+France): it cautions against reliance on false Adhémar charters when discussing early Guillaume-Hugues traditions. Assigning a confidently numbered “Giraud II” or “Guillaume-Hugues” in 1101 would exceed the checked evidence. [Amis du Vieux Donzère, *Le Val des Nymphes*](https://amisduvieuxdonzere.com/docs/Revues/RD_06_80%21Le_val_des_nymphes.pdf).

**Holdings and culture.** The castle complex provides firmer chronology than distant ancestry. The department dates its chapel to the **eleventh century**, but the prestigious palace and great hall to the **twelfth century**, under Géraud Adhémar. Their southern route influence was becoming materially visible. The chapel supports a religious dimension to lordship; the later hall supports elite display, without proving that this palace existed in 1101. [Drôme departmental heritage inventory](https://cartepatrimoine.ladrome.fr/notice-1271).

**Colors, arms and motto.** No verified 1101 palette, arms or motto. The later shield is **gold with three blue diagonal bands**. An official inventory identifies those arms on baptismal fonts dated **1687** in [Grignan](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Grignan%2C+France). This is a securely dated later object, not the origin of the arms. Popular mottos cannot be assigned to the 1101 household from that object. [French Ministry of Culture, Palissy IM26000588](https://pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/palissy/IM26000588).

**Story use:** a rising southern castle family is defensible; a precise 1101 dynasty, extensive later branch holdings and a completed Romanesque palace need qualification.

## 4. Arnaud: the early lords of Crest

**Existence, leadership and holdings.** [Crest](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Crest%2C+Dr%C3%B4me%2C+France) is directly relevant to the story. The municipality attributes its origin to the Arnaud family and the name *Crista Arnaldorum*. Its retrospective account places their ownership before **1145** and identifies a papal letter of **2 March 1120** as a castle reference. These support an early lordship; they do not identify a named Arnaud ruling in autumn 1101. [Crest municipal history](https://www.mairie-crest.fr/decouvrir-me-divertir/patrimoine/histoire-de-la-ville).

**Culture and church relationships.** The town’s account describes an allod, which warns against treating all land as a fief of Albon. A defensible story household controls a local strongpoint, settlement and associated resources. Its precise rights, relations with the bishop of [Die](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Die%2C+Dr%C3%B4me%2C+France), and 1101 succession remain unverified here. The present monumental tower’s final form must not be used as a plan for 1101.

**Colors, arms and motto.** No verified 1101 evidence. Later municipal arms are not proof of Arnaud family arms. Similar names in later armorials do not establish continuity.

**Story use:** the historical early lordship supports the location. Lambert, the story’s pseudo-bishop of Crest, remains fictional; this research does not supply him a historical episcopal office or pedigree.

## 5. Poitiers-Valentinois: important later, uncertain in 1101

**Existence and formation.** Do not treat the later counts of Valentinois from the house of Poitiers as securely established rulers in 1101. Their early chronology is difficult, and the territorial county must be distinguished from the later dynasty. A charter of **Aymar I of Poitiers** for [Léoncel abbey](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Abbaye+de+L%C3%A9oncel%2C+France) is catalogued within **1125–1178**, not 1101. The date range is broad and does not by itself prove accession in 1125. [IRHT, Léoncel cartulary III](https://telma.irht.cnrs.fr/chartes/136718).

**Leadership, holdings and culture.** No secure Poitiers household head for 1101 was established. Later, their power became significant in the [Valentinois](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Valentinois%2C+France) and at Crest. Crest’s general history gives **1145** for succession, but its more specific heraldry page dates a **partial transfer to 1165**. Use that qualified account; do not imply a single securely established transfer date. [General history](https://www.mairie-crest.fr/decouvrir-me-divertir/patrimoine/histoire-de-la-ville), [specific Poitiers account](https://www.mairie-crest.fr/decouvrir-me-divertir/patrimoine/histoire-de-la-ville/armoiries-des-poitiers).

The Léoncel charter shows Aymar protecting the abbey’s pack animals and handlers because supplies must travel into difficult mountain country. Charity and a lord’s threat against interference operate together. This example belongs to the later twelfth century. [IRHT charter](https://telma.irht.cnrs.fr/chartes/136718).

**Colors, arms and motto.** No verified 1101 evidence. The later palette is **blue with six silver roundels in rows of 3–2–1, beneath a gold chief**, explicitly given in Crest’s present heritage account. This color attribution is retrospective, not derived from wax. [Municipal heraldry page](https://www.mairie-crest.fr/decouvrir-me-divertir/patrimoine/histoire-de-la-ville/armoiries-des-poitiers). Aymar IV’s **1278–1288** seal shows roundels beneath a chief. Sigilla’s associated entry specifies **six**; the seal description’s word “seven” conflicts with the 3–2–1 arrangement. No verified 1101 motto. [Dated seal](https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/3884), [linked armorial record](https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/152046).

**Story use:** retain them as future context. A fully developed Poitiers-Valentinois court in 1101 would need new evidence or an explicit fictional choice.

## 6. Clermont: before the name Clermont-Tonnerre

**Attestation and leadership.** This is the Dauphinois family associated with the [Chirens–Paladru area](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Chirens%2C+Is%C3%A8re%2C+France), not every unrelated Clermont family. The family’s historical website identifies **Siboud/Sibaud I** as a witness in **1094** to an agreement involving the count of Burgundy and the archbishop of [Vienne](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Vienne%2C+Is%C3%A8re%2C+France). This is a useful attestation claim, but this research did not independently inspect that original act. It does not settle whether the same man headed the house in 1101. [Family historical account](https://clermont-tonnerre.fr/fr/index.php?menu=histoire).

The acquisition of [Tonnerre](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Tonnerre%2C+Yonne%2C+France) came through a **1496** marriage, and the compound surname is later. Use “Clermont” in 1101. Later dignity as a leading barony does not prove an identical rank or territorial extent in that year. [Family genealogy and name history](https://www.clermont-tonnerre.fr/fr/personnages.php?menu=histoire).

**Culture and relationships.** Witnessing such an agreement places the early family in aristocratic and episcopal networks. Claims of participation in the First Crusade are less secure. Treat them as family tradition unless a contemporary witness is found; likewise, do not convert later religious foundations into 1101 possessions.

**Colors, arms and motto.** No verified 1101 evidence. The familiar later arms are **two silver keys crossed on red**. The family heraldry account cites a **1271** seal of Aynard with crossed keys, but that seal was not independently located in an archive catalogue here. A supposed primitive mountain-and-sun design has unknown colors and is explicitly conjectural. [Family heraldic account](https://clermont-tonnerre.fr/fr/index.php?menu=heraldique).

The story of a papal grant in **1120 or 1121**, rewarding Siboud for saving the pope, is disputed even in that family account. It cannot explain an authenticated 1101 shield. The later motto, given there as **“Etiam si omnes ego no,”** means approximately “Even if everyone else does, I will not,” associated with Peter’s declaration of loyalty. No 1101 use is verified. [Family history](https://clermont-tonnerre.fr/fr/index.php?menu=histoire).

**Story use:** a northern regional lordly family is plausible. Papal keys, tiara, rescue story and exalted later rank require visible chronological limits.

## 7. Clérieu: an established lower-Isère lineage

**Existence, leadership and holdings.** The town of [Clérieux](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Cl%C3%A9rieux%2C+France) traces its lords to **Silvion around 930**, then names **Guillaume I in 994** and **Silvion II in 1123**. This retrospective sequence supports a long-standing lineage near [Romans](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Romans-sur-Is%C3%A8re%2C+France), but leaves the 1101 household head unresolved. Its later barony’s full list of settlements must not be treated as a surveyed 1101 estate. [Clérieux municipal history](https://www.clerieux.fr/fr/histoire-de-cl%C3%A9rieux_r_116.html).

**Culture and influence.** Its location makes the family relevant to travel between Royans, the lower Isère and the Rhône. The town’s account describes close links with the church of Saint-Barnard, without establishing specific acts of patronage in 1101. The suggested descent from ancient Roman families is an origin claim, not a verified continuous genealogy. [Municipal history](https://www.clerieux.fr/fr/histoire-de-cl%C3%A9rieux_r_116.html).

**Colors, arms and motto.** No verified 1101 palette or motto. **Roger III’s seal, used in 1288–1292**, shows a **single diagonal band**. It is catalogued from Haute-Savoie archives SA 63.10 and the National Archives J 277, no. 4. The description does not specify colors. It must not be replaced silently by a popular online two-key attribution or by Clermont’s crossed-key design. [Sigilla, Roger III de Clérieu](https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/3846).

**Story use:** Clérieu is a defensible established regional presence. Assigning a named 1101 lord remains a deliberate fictional decision.

## 8. Humbertians: the neighboring power later called Savoy

**Existence and leadership.** The dynasty arose under Humbert I in the early eleventh century. **Humbert II** rules in 1101 and dies in **1103**; Amédée III follows him. The archive regesta place Humbert II’s records in 1092–1103, which is an attestation span rather than necessarily his complete reign. [Digi-Archives, regesta index](https://www.digi-archives.org/fonds/edit-gen/static/table.html).

**Holdings and culture.** Their power lay in [Savoy](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Savoie%2C+France), [Maurienne](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Maurienne%2C+France) and Alpine routes extending toward [Aosta](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Aosta%2C+Italy) and [Susa](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Susa%2C+Italy). Their interests form northern and eastern context rather than direct rule over the whole Drôme corridor.

A **1097** charter calls Humbert a count and marquis, records a gift to the priory at [Le Bourget-du-Lac](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Le+Bourget-du-Lac%2C+France), and states that he is preparing to go overseas. The priory supplies a fine mule in return. This is concrete evidence of religious patronage joined to travel and useful transport; it is not proof that he reached the First Crusade. [IRHT, Cluny charter 3727](https://telma.irht.cnrs.fr/chartes/127098).

**Colors, arms and motto.** No verified 1101 palette or motto. Later Savoy arms are **a silver cross on red**. Amédée V’s **1297** seal provides a dated archival example of the cross, with the cataloguer’s later blazon. This does not establish the first use. Retrospective traditions about eagle arms, crusading rewards and the cross must not supply Humbert II with an assumed 1101 uniform. [Sigilla, National Archives J 631, no. 16](https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/34764), [municipal library discussion of the competing traditions](https://www.guichetdusavoir.org/question/voir/29082).

**Story use:** a growing neighboring dynasty, useful for Alpine communications and political competition, without a mature later Savoy state.

## 9. Raymondines: Toulouse and Saint-Gilles

**Existence and leadership.** **Raymond IV of Saint-Gilles**, of the established comital dynasty, is alive and active in 1101. His power had accumulated through family inheritance and claims reaching from [Toulouse](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Toulouse%2C+France) toward [Saint-Gilles](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Saint-Gilles%2C+Gard%2C+France) and [Provence](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Provence%2C+France). The details of when particular titles were acquired vary between reconstructions. Monique Zerner’s study stresses inheritance, shared comital power and Saint-Gilles’s position at the southern end of the Rhône route. [Zerner, “Le comte de Toulouse Raymond IV chef de peuple,” p. 46](https://www.persee.fr/doc/efr_0000-0000_1993_act_168_1_4332?pageId=T1_46).

He led a major contingent in the **First Crusade of 1096–1099**, remained in the eastern Mediterranean, and joined the disastrous **1101** expedition in Anatolia. His son Bertrand handled interests at home, amid contested control. Raymond should not appear casually holding a local autumn court in Royans. [Biographical synthesis and scholarly references](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_IV%2C_Count_of_Toulouse).

**Culture and regional influence.** Crusade leadership, religious claims and aristocratic inheritance make this house especially relevant to returning southern crusaders. Its Provençal interests do not prove direct lordship over every settlement on the story’s route. A returning soldier could invoke Raymond’s authority or remember rival leaders; the narrator should distinguish those memories from local legal rights.

**Colors, arms and motto.** No verified 1101 family palette or motto. Later Toulouse arms use a **gold, hollow, key-headed cross with twelve terminal balls on red**. Raymond VI’s **1204** seal shows the cross on his shield, with a star and crescent in the field. [Sigilla, National Archives J 589, no. 1](https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/7971).

A supposed armorial bull of Raymond IV dated **1088** is a recognized forgery. It cannot authenticate this cross for 1101. Henri Rolland’s study distinguishes later twelfth-century coin and seal evidence from that false early witness. [Rolland, “L’origine provençale de la Croix de Toulouse,” p. 54](https://www.persee.fr/doc/prohi_0033-1856_1950_num_1_1_3510?pageId=T1_54).

## What is established, rising or still in the future?

**Established:** regional aristocratic lineages, inherited church patronage, count’s courts, local castle rights and negotiations over tithes and land. Royans’s princely lineage and Clérieu’s local history belong to this background. Church institutions are active property holders and political actors, not scenery.

**Rising:** Albon’s territorial power, Humbertian control across Alpine routes, and the southern castle influence later expressed by the Adhémar palace. Their later territories should not replace the less uniform political geography of 1101.

**Future:** the Dauphin designation beginning in 1110; a settled hereditary armorial system; the Poitiers-Valentinois ruling position documented later; the fourteenth-century Bérenger-Sassenage union; and the later name Clermont-Tonnerre. These dates constrain presentation without requiring any change to the canon.

For 1101 costume and banners, a personal symbol, a religious sign or a deliberately chosen cloth color can be an artistic choice. Record that choice as fiction where there is no period witness. Do not give every later house a matching shield, tabard, horse cloth, household livery and motto solely because a modern armorial supplies colors.

## Remaining evidence gaps

The principal unresolved issue is **individual succession**, especially the 1101 head of Royans, early Sassenage, Monteil, Arnaud, Clermont and Clérieu. The circa 1040 Royans notice proves an earlier prince; it does not identify Guarin. Guillaume of Sassenage’s court case retains a doubtful date. Clermont’s 1094 witness claim and 1271 seal need direct archival confirmation. Monteil pedigrees require discrimination between authentic and false charters. Poitiers-Valentinois needs a securely dated early charter before any 1101 ruling role can be asserted. No house studied here has a verified 1101 family motto. These are precise limits on the evidence, rather than invitations to turn later legend into contemporary fact.
