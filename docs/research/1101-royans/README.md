# Historical research for Dias Irae in 1101

These six reports examine the people, institutions, beliefs, environment, and material culture around the story. They use the campaign's existing date, from late summer into winter 1101. The aim is to give the story and art team a usable historical base, with clear boundaries between evidence, later tradition, and deliberate invention.

The setting comes from [the story outline](../../../story/story-arch.md) and [the location dossiers](../../../story/locations.md). Those documents establish the fiction; the sources linked in the research reports establish historical claims. This research does not change the story files.

The [England comparison](../1101-england/README.md) examines supported differences for the same 1101 story season.

## How to read the evidence

**Documented** means that an opened source supports the claim at the date and place stated. **Disputed** means that the evidence or scholarly interpretation differs. **Later tradition** identifies an account recorded after the events, often to explain a family's prestige or an institution's origin. **Story inference** identifies a proposed use or plausible reconstruction, rather than an attested local event.

An institution can exist before it receives its later rule, military role, costume, or emblem. A noble family can exist before its familiar coat of arms is recorded. A religious accusation establishes what an accuser said; it does not establish that the alleged ritual occurred. These distinctions apply throughout the dossier.

Dates for sky events follow the calendar convention stated in the climate report. Reconstructed eclipses and climate records are different kinds of evidence from eyewitness accounts. Neither establishes the weather in a particular village on a particular day without local records.

## The period at a glance

This table summarizes the dated evidence in the linked reports. A wider development does not establish immediate adoption in the campaign's villages.

| Area | Relevant in 1101 | Ascendant, continuing, or waning |
|---|---|---|
| [Religious institutions](christian-orders.md) | Monasteries, the early Antonine alms community, and Gérard's hospital. | New charitable and reform communities are developing. The familiar military orders and many of their symbols belong to later stages. Cluny remains vigorous. |
| [Noble power](noble-houses.md) | Families, estates, church connections, and overlapping local authority. | Albon's regional power is developing. Familiar hereditary arms, mottoes, and later titles must be dated separately. Albon's authority does not extend over every lordship in the story corridor. |
| [Religious plurality](religions-and-rumors.md) | Jewish communities, Muslim societies elsewhere in Europe, and pagan traditions on parts of Europe's northern and eastern margins. | Christian expansion constrains some pagan traditions. Ancient Mithraism survives as material remains; a local living cult is unverified. Later occult accusations are separate evidence. |
| [Environment](climate-and-sky.md) | Seasonal dependence, uneven valley and upland exposure, and memories of recent scarcity. | Broad climate trends coexist with cold winters. No verified local weather series establishes a change of regime in 1101. Latin access to translated astrology expands over the next century. |
| [Daily technology](technology-and-provisions.md) | Mills, hand tools, skilled crafts, mail, horses, crossbows, and stored food. | Mill networks and medical translations develop. Household methods continue beside them; no local technological collapse is documented. |
| [Art and sound](music-and-visual-culture.md) | Chant, early instruments, painted religious space, decorated books, layered clothing, and Romanesque building. | Musical teaching and early Occitan lyric develop. Romanesque art is active; Gothic, the mature troubadour repertoire, and the familiar complete *Dies irae* sequence lie later. |

## Story choices to review

The reports identify places where the story uses a later institution, an uncertain practice, or invented history. These are choices for the author; the research leaves the canon intact.

| Canon element | Evidence boundary and usable choice |
|---|---|
| Six-year-old Antonine “order,” widespread tau marks, armed commandery, and detailed treatments | The [orders report](christian-orders.md) distinguishes the early house of alms from later organization, badges, and treatment traditions. Keep the armed faction and precise visual program as deliberate invention. |
| Regional family colors, mottoes, and titles | The [houses report](noble-houses.md) supplies dated later references. Several 1101 household heads remain unknown. A chosen fictional palette can be recorded without claiming an attested hereditary livery. |
| Local pagan or Satanic networks | The [religions report](religions-and-rumors.md) distinguishes living religions, hostile descriptions, ancient material remains, and later occult legends. The canon's local supernatural network has an invented history. |
| Mithraic shrine beneath the granary | A regional ancient analogy supports the setting's visual vocabulary. The precise shrine and earlier supernatural sealing remain fiction; see [Mithraism and Roman remains](religions-and-rumors.md#mithraism-and-roman-remains-strong-material-possibility-no-medieval-continuity). |
| Apollin equated with Abaddon | The [religions report](religions-and-rumors.md#satanic-covens-baphomet-and-other-later-constructions) treats the poem's gods as literary polemic and the equation as an interpretation that needs its own argument. |
| Damp weather and a dramatic sky omen | Damp conditions remain a plausible fictional season. The [climate report](climate-and-sky.md) finds no visible 1101 solar eclipse at the two representative local points and no total lunar eclipse that year. A conspicuous red Moon or local daytime darkness needs an invented cause or another date. |
| Illness spreading through the campaign | The [provisions report](technology-and-provisions.md) connects historical ergot poisoning to contaminated food, including old stores. Human contact does not transmit the toxin. Supernatural spread is a separate story choice. |
| Cider stores and military equipment | Local cider production, standard integrated mail mittens, and universal thick quilted garments remain unverified here. The [technology report](technology-and-provisions.md) supplies dated equipment references and plausible household alternatives. |
| Music and surviving buildings | The [visual-culture report](music-and-visual-culture.md) distinguishes older funeral language from the later complete *Dies irae*, and early institutions from later surviving masonry. It retains the canon's stated Isenheim inspiration while supplying contemporary references for objects within scenes. |

**Flagellant bands need a separate date distinction.** Eleventh-century monastic self-scourging is evidenced, including Peter Damian and Dominicus Loricatus. The organized public flagellant movement associated with Raniero Fasani begins in **1260**. The story can use an invented early local band, while retaining older penitential practice as its background. It should not borrow the later movement's full organization and song culture as documented 1101 life. [Marco Piana, *Fonte de Pietade*, pp. 29–30 and note 11](https://jps.library.utoronto.ca/index.php/confrat/article/download/33688/25855/82012#page=3).

## Research record

Six topic subagents produced the six reports. The parent reviewed their work and checked the key evidence. The [scope and verification record](research-notes.md) lists each assignment, the project checkpoint, assumptions, review checks, and remaining limits. The dossier supplies a supported range of references; it does not claim an exhaustive census of every household, belief, practice, or object in the region.
