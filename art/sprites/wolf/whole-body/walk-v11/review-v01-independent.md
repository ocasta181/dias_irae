## Independent assessment

The complete-sheet approach improves character continuity. There are no comparably severe three-frame identity resets, all frames show an organically connected grey-brown wolf, the weary eye/ears/muzzle remain consistent, and the camera keeps facing right. Those are useful gains to preserve as method evidence, not reasons to promote this animation.

The user's whole-body requirement is still unmet. Across the exact pairs the muzzle angle, ears, eye, cheek/ruff outline, major back/coat bands and tail remain nearly fixed while limbs swing. Some small vertical drift and local changes at thigh/chest connections exist; it would be inaccurate to claim literally no body pixels move. Those changes do not read as coordinated shoulder/chest compression, haunch rotation, abdominal deformation, neck/head counter-movement or fur/tail response. A very small planned center excursion also cannot by itself demonstrate articulation. This review cannot determine whether the under-1.3-unit target center changes are numerically followed; it can determine that the visible deformation does not convey the intended whole-animal gait.

The paw plan is not reliably followed. The most decisive exposed front-leg problem is E-07→08 retreat during the planned RF forward swing, followed by the abrupt forward-looking change in E-09. E-03/04 and E-10…12 also conflict with the near RF stance lane/trajectory. Far LF advancement in E-01…03 is not independently readable where its planned path should emerge, and front/back paw lanes become ambiguous. Natural overlap is allowed; ambiguous overlap does not prove a hidden guide match. A calm approved four-beat walk cannot be claimed from this sheet.

Under the fixed registration, the painted nose extends farther right than the head/muzzle endpoint and the furry tail/body extend farther left than the control. The animal consequently reads wider/longer and more horizontal than the tiny squat volume target. Relative to the original identity, the head appears less dominant over the extended back. These are visual proportion/camera-envelope concerns; exact dimensions and any permissible tolerance belong to the parent's independent measurement. Per-frame rescaling/recentering would conceal the problem rather than solve it.

At 12→01, coat/head identity changes less than in V10, but the front support trajectory still does not close convincingly. An image grid and static chronological inspection do not verify actual 12-frame timing, one-second cycle, ground contact or runtime playback. No numerical world-contact, normal/slow-speed export playback or input-transition pass is claimed.
