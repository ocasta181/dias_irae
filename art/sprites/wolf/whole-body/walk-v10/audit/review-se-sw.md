## Verdict

**Both cycles fail the requested whole-body-motion review and remain candidates.** The intact silhouettes retain a recognizably stocky wolf, large head, pointed ears, short legs, pale muzzle, dark back, and continuous neck/torso. SE and SW remain recognizable down-right and down-left views. I did not see an isolated floating head or an obvious detached painted component.

However, the ears, face, ruff, spine contour, tail contour, and coat pattern appear held within each three-frame block while the legs change. At block boundaries, the body/head envelope and registration change abruptly. Those changes read as different held drawings, rather than weight transfer passing continuously through the animal. Coat detail moves with neither the shoulder loading nor the advancing limb inside a block. Continuous pixels alone therefore do not satisfy the whole-body requirement.

The approved overlay also misses the painted anatomy throughout both cycles: the neck/head path falls below the painted muzzle, and the shoulder/spine path runs low through the lower chest/underside rather than following the corresponding painted anatomy. Several visible paw endpoints lie above or laterally away from their target endpoints. This is an observed registration/pose mismatch, not a numerical contact measurement.

## Cycle findings and repair priorities

1. **Replace the frozen upper-body authoring rule for every SE/SW frame.** Keeping identity, proportions, and recognizable coat markings consistent is compatible with drawing the whole wolf at every phase. Keeping the head/spine artwork identical while changing only limbs contradicts this task. Redraw connected shoulder/pelvis loading, chest and neck response, small head orientation changes, and fur flow that belongs to that anatomy. Keep the ground root and approved support targets as constraints; a runtime bob cannot repair these drawings.
2. **Resolve registration and body geometry before polishing feet.** The same-coordinate overlays show a repeatable head/neck/chest mismatch in all 24 frames, plus displaced hind and forepaws. Establish the painted shoulder, hip, neck connection, muzzle, and visible paw landmarks against the approved plan on the common canvas. Preserve the original identity reference. Do not independently align/rescale each wolf or substitute planned coordinates for measured pixels. If the existing guide needs extra upper-body articulation to express the new requirement, make that explicit rather than silently changing its approved contacts.
3. **Repair the clearest touchdown discontinuities first: SE 03→04 and SW 09→10.** The advancing front paw jumps back at a moment when its approved target changes little. Correct the complete adjacent whole-body poses together, including the shoulder/chest, head, and overlap. Then inspect SE 06→07, 09→10, and 12→01; SW 03→04, 06→07, and 12→01. All four three-frame boundaries in each direction change the upper-body envelope, so fixing only one paw will leave visible jumps.
4. **Make the support transfer and hidden-limb topology readable.** Particular uncertainties are SE 03–06 and 09–12, and SW 03–06 and 09–12. Use anatomical-side/occlusion controls for the hind swing and the under-chest front leg. A hidden foot may legitimately remain hidden, but its connecting limb and overlap must support the intended gait. Do not invent an exact position for an invisible paw or expose every far limb merely to satisfy a count.
5. **Review each repaired full cycle as one continuous animal.** Stable coat markings should deform with the torso and shoulder rather than hold in three-frame groups or redraw abruptly between them. Tail motion should connect to the rump and settle continuously through 12→01. After the redraw, annotate actual visible paws/joints and evaluate world contacts using actual root travel; then inspect exported playback at normal/slow speed and gameplay scale. Contact results and appearance results must remain separate.
