## Cycle findings

**N:** The clearest hind action is the alternating flexed/extended pair in N-04…06 and N-10…12. It is undermined by N-09's wrong hind configuration, weak stance progression in adjacent frames, and mostly unreadable front motion. Rear-view occlusion is legitimate, but this drawing cannot prove the hidden front contacts or swings. The back, head and tail appear held inside 01…03, 04…06, 07…09 and 10…12. At 03→04 the head/rump/tail proportions change visibly; 06→07 and 09→10 change the coat and upper silhouette; 12→01 is a clear broad-head/short-tail reset. These jumps read as four separately drawn animal states, not support-linked whole-body motion.

**S:** Foreleg length changes make the intended alternating advance clearer than in N. S-01 and S-07 have useful asymmetric support contrast; S-08's near-level pair broadly follows the control. That does not excuse RF stance retraction in 02/03, held LF stance in 04…06, prematurely shortened LF in 09, or LF moving downward as its swing begins in 12. Hind limbs are mostly hidden by the head. The lateral paw in 10…12 needs anatomical annotation before it can be claimed as RH. Front head/back/tail contours hold inside the same four trios, while 03→04, 06→07 and 09→10 change muzzle/torso/fur design. At 12→01 the upper design reset is smaller than N's but still visible; the paw appearance/occlusion and endpoint displacement do not close cleanly.

**Whole-body requirement:** The animal is connected in every still, but connected alpha and recognizable anatomy do not establish coordinated gait. Across both cycles, there is no convincing phase-linked head/neck response, shoulder/rump compression, torso balance or tail response within each trio. Major fur bands remain still while legs change, then jump at the authoring boundaries. This is a visual outcome, not proof that source images were assembled from parts. The recorded N/S authoring instruction, “Keep the head/spine/camera/scale and coat features identical across all three; only the specified limbs change,” explicitly encourages the held-upper-body failure and needs correction before another attempt.

## Repair priorities and limits

1. Replace that limb-only authoring instruction. Plan a small set of intact support-driven whole-body keys first: coordinated neck/head, chest/shoulder, flank/hip and tail response, stable coat markings moving with their skin/fur, and the same approved identity, camera, root, proportions and paw targets. Paint each animal intact. No painted-part assembly, runtime bob or held head/torso can satisfy this requirement.
2. Repair the clearest target errors first: N-08→09→10 hind ordering; S-01→02→03 RF stance; S-04→05→06 LF stance progression; S-08→09→10 LF support; and S-11→12→01 LF low swing/RF stance. Use the exact paired controls, not a nearby pose. Recheck every changed neighbor and seam.
3. Repair all four trio boundaries for identity, coat and body continuity. Preserve the original base appearance rather than accepting a failed group as identity authority. Do not independently recenter or resize frames to hide jumps.
4. Annotate actual visible underside landmarks and attachment points on the resulting drawings. Mark occluded N forepaws and S hind paws as occluded; do not substitute target coordinates for measurements. Resolve the lateral S-10…12 paw's identity and lane. Only then can a numerical world-contact check be made.
5. After whole-body and pose repairs, inspect the exported atlas at normal and slow speed, actual display scale, and stationary-ground/moving-root playback, including start/stop and 12→01. This review covers static chronological visual comparisons only; it does not verify runtime timing, contact drift, input transitions, alpha connectivity quantitatively or engine integration.

No exact painted-paw coordinates were measured. Depth/overlap and short furry limbs make some joints and underside landmarks uncertain. A plausible endpoint order is not a passed planted-contact check. All 24 frames remain candidates pending repair, independent measurement and motion playback.
