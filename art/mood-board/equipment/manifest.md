# Dress and equipment review manifest

**23 new internet references, all pending review.** This supplement does not change the approval of the original 26-image mood board. Select individual construction, silhouette and material choices before the remaining cast concepts. An accepted photograph is a design reference, not automatic adoption of every detail or whole-board approval.

Open [the review gallery](index.html). Every view supports Accept, Reject, Revise, Pending and written commentary. Download the manifest and save it as `art/mood-board/equipment/manifest.md`, or give your selections in chat. Browser drafts do not edit repository files.

The story is set in 1101. Guarin’s full-face greathelm is the explicitly permitted anachronism. Later armor, shield and dress examples are labeled for comparison; selecting one deliberately can authorize a further design exception. Most entries are photographs of real objects or reconstructions. E22 is a photographed historical sculpture, and E23 is a garment detail; neither is a complete surviving dress. No generated equipment images are included.

## Greathelm shapes

| Identifier | Choice | Evidence and design use | Image | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E01 | Flat barrel greathelm | Surviving helmet · later 13th century. Plain flat crown, narrow slit, riveted bands: the closest real object to Guarin’s current helmet. Later than 1101. Guarin’s greathelm anachronism is explicitly allowed; compare its shape rather than copying corrosion. | [E01](images/e01-flat-barrel.jpg) | pending |  |
| E02 | Rounded greathelm | Surviving helmet · around 1300. Softer rounded crown and enclosed face provide a different helmet silhouette. Later than 1101. Compare crown and visor construction; the game still needs a dark, compact silhouette. | [E02](images/e02-rounded-greathelm.jpg) | pending |  |
| E03 | Raised-crown greathelm | Surviving helmet · mid 14th century. Raised crown offers a taller, sharper alternative to the flat barrel. Later than 1101. The crown shape is an optional design choice, not an approved change to Guarin. | [E03](images/e03-raised-crown.jpg) | pending |  |

## Body armor and layers

| E04 | Plain mail shirt | Surviving mail shirt · object date unverified here. Small linked rings, soft drape and a simple shirt outline for Guarin’s body armor. ## Weapon forms

| Identifier | Choice | Evidence and design use | Image | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E07 | Straight cruciform sword | Surviving sword · possibly 12th–early 13th century. Straight double-edged blade, simple crossguard and a restrained hilt: a clear arming-sword silhouette. Slightly later comparison. Keep the blade and grip on a rigid straight axis in concepts; do not reproduce damage as a crooked weapon. | [E07](images/e07-straight-sword.jpg) | pending |  |
| E08 | Lobed-pommel sword | Museum sword display · exact date unverified here. Lobed pommel and decorated hilt offer an older-looking alternative to the simple cruciform hilt. Several swords are pictured. Select the hilt treatment deliberately; northern decoration is not automatically appropriate for rural France. | [E08](images/e08-lobed-pommel.jpg) | pending |  |
| E09 | Broad axe head | Surviving axe head · Scandinavian, 11th–12th century. Broad steel cutting edge and a compact socket give the axe a very different action silhouette from a sword. Head only; the haft is not pictured. Silver ornament is a wealth ceiling, not the default for an ordinary work axe. | [E09](images/e09-broad-axe.jpg) | pending |  |
| E10 | Socketed spearhead | Archaeological find · probable medieval date. A narrow iron point and tubular socket give a simple spear a readable construction. The source gives a broad probable medieval range, not a firm 1101 date. Rust and the modern measuring scale are not design details. | [E10](images/e10-leaf-spear.jpg) | pending |  |

## Priests and religious dress

| Identifier | Choice | Evidence and design use | Image | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E13 | Long liturgical tunic | Surviving silk liturgical garment · approximately 11th century. Long plain overall form and restrained drape for distinguishing a priest from a fighter. Silk ceremonial tunic with later replacement lining, not an ordinary linen alb. Giraud’s grey alb remains the story starting point. | [E13](images/e13-liturgical-tunic.jpg) | pending |  |
| E14 | Ceremonial chasuble | Surviving chasuble · approximately 1330–50. A broad outer vestment and its embroidered panels separate ceremonial priestly dress from an everyday robe. Much later and very rich. The photo shows the laid-out vestment, not its worn drape; embroidery is a ceremony-only wealth ceiling. | [E14](images/e14-bell-chasuble.jpg) | pending |  |
| E15 | Plain work habit | Photograph of a working monk · 1950. Unarmored robe, layered cowl and cloth weight in an ordinary working pose, useful for Odo’s habit. Modern cut reference, not 1101 evidence. The black-and-white photo does not establish garment color or show every front detail. | [E15](images/e15-dark-habit.jpg) | pending |  |
| E16 | Ceremonial cope | Surviving mantle · approximately 1150–1200. A sweeping outer cape gives clergy a distinct ceremonial silhouette without body armor. Later regional ceremonial garment. Rich blue and embroidery are not a default for the rural parish or Odo’s black/taupe habit. | [E16](images/e16-ceremonial-cope.jpg) | pending |  |

| E20 | Travel cloak and hood | Museum display of surviving clothing · 14th century. Tunic, separate hood and cloak offer practical travel layers and a compact outer silhouette. Later comparison. Do not treat the whole ensemble as an 1101 costume; choose useful cloak/hood forms separately. | [E20](images/e20-travel-cloak.jpg) | pending |  |
